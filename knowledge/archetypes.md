# Mechanism Families (Archetypes) & How Games Blend Them

Classify games by **mechanism** (how they work), not theme. Most modern games combine several
families. Below: crisp definitions + canonical reference games, then how blends work.

## Core mechanism families

### Worker Placement
Place a limited pool of workers onto action spaces; spaces are usually **blocked** once taken, so
players compete for the best actions (action denial + turn-order tension).
*Refs:* **Agricola** (the archetype), **Lords of Waterdeep** (accessible), **Viticulture**, **Caverna**, **Stone Age**.

### Deck-Building
Start with an identical small deck; **acquire cards** from a shared market into your deck as you play.
The deck itself is the engine.
*Refs:* **Dominion** (invented it, SdJ 2009), **Star Realms**/**Ascension** (duel), **Clank!** (deck-build + push-your-luck crawl).

### Engine-Building
Assemble interlocking pieces so resources/actions **compound** over time. Deck-building and tableau-building are subtypes.
*Refs:* **Wingspan**, **Terraforming Mars**, **Gizmos**, **Splendor**.

### Area Control / Area Majority
Compete for **presence/majority in regions**; score whoever has the most influence at scoring moments.
*Refs:* **El Grande** (classic), **Blood Rage**, **Kemet**, **Twilight Struggle** (influence).

### Tile-Laying
Place tiles into a spatial layout; score from adjacency and pattern.
*Refs:* **Carcassonne** (archetype, SdJ 2001), **Azul** (SdJ 2018), **Cascadia** (SdJ 2022).

### Drafting (Card / Dice)
**Select from a shared/passed pool**, denying options to others. Card drafting often uses pick-and-pass; dice drafting picks from a rolled pool.
*Refs:* **7 Wonders** (simultaneous pick-and-pass), **Sushi Go!** (teaching), **Sagrada**/**Castles of Burgundy** (dice).

### Set Collection
Score by gathering **specified combinations** (matching, diverse, or graduated).
*Refs:* **Ticket to Ride** (SdJ 2004), **Sushi Go!**, **Ra**, **Splendor**.

### Roll-and-Write / Flip-and-Write
Shared randomizer (dice/cards); each player **marks a personal sheet**. Near-zero interaction, high parallelism, low downtime.
*Refs:* **Qwixx**, **Ganz schön clever** ("That's Pretty Clever"), **Welcome To...** (flip-and-write), **Cartographers**.

### Cooperative
All players **win or lose together** vs the system. Two hard problems to solve by design: the
**alpha-player/"quarterbacking"** problem (one dominant player runs everyone's turn) and building a
**compelling opponent from rules alone** (the system must *push back* — a timer, escalating threat, or
automa-like adversary).
*Refs:* **Pandemic** (archetype), **Spirit Island** (heavy, asymmetric), **The Crew** (co-op trick-taking).

- **Constrained-communication co-op** (a studio favourite — see `designer-profile.md`): the design
  *limits what players may tell each other*, so the **information bottleneck becomes the core fun** —
  and it neatly dissolves the alpha-player problem (no one can dictate; you must infer). *Refs:*
  **Hanabi** (hidden own-hand, coded hints), **Sky Team** (no table talk, simultaneous placement),
  **SAIL** (no talking), **The Crew** (only limited signalling). Design levers: hidden hands, a tiny
  legal-communication vocabulary, simultaneous reveals, and pressure that punishes over-coordination.
- **Co-op + progression:** deckbuilding/campaign co-ops (**Slay the Spire: TBG**, **Gloomhaven**,
  **Aeon's End**) fight solvability with variable setups and an escalating, evolving adversary.

### Campaign / Legacy
Play persists across sessions. **Legacy** permanently alters components (stickers, tearing, writing) and unlocks content — an evolving, one-time narrative. Campaigns may reset/replay.
*Refs:* **Pandemic Legacy: S1** (landmark), **Gloomhaven**/**Frosthaven**, **Risk Legacy** (originated it, 2011).

### Deduction / Social Deduction
Deduction = infer hidden info from clues (logic puzzle). Social deduction = hidden roles/teams + bluffing.
*Refs:* Deduction — **Cluedo**, **Sherlock Holmes Consulting Detective**. Social — **The Resistance/Avalon**, **Werewolf**, **Secret Hitler**, **Blood on the Clocktower**.

### Dexterity
Physical skill — flicking, stacking, balancing, timing.
*Refs:* **Jenga**, **Crokinole** (flicking; highly ranked), **Klask**, **Flick 'em Up!**.

### Auction / Bidding
Bid resources for items/positions. Variants: open outcry, once-around, sealed, Dutch (descending), turn-order auctions.
*Refs:* **Ra**, **Modern Art** (Knizia masterclasses), **Power Grid** (auction in an economic game).

### Network / Route Building
Build **connected networks** (rail/roads/pipes); value from connectivity and reach.
*Refs:* **Ticket to Ride** (accessible), **Brass: Birmingham** (networks + economy, BGG #1), **18xx** (heavy rail).

### Hand Management
Skill is **timing and sequencing** cards (each has multiple uses / opportunity cost).
*Refs:* **Race for the Galaxy**, **San Juan**, **Concordia**.

### Push-Your-Luck
**Press for more reward at rising risk** of busting.
*Refs:* **Can't Stop**, **Incan Gold/Diamant**, **Quacks of Quedlinburg** (bag-build push-your-luck).

### Action Point / Action Selection
Budget of action points spent on a menu (APA), or choose from a shared/limited menu (rondel, wheel, programming).
*Refs:* **Pandemic** (4 actions/turn), **Tzolk'in**, **Concordia**/**Antike** (rondel).

### Trick-Taking
Each player plays one card to a trick; highest (often follow-suit) wins. Endlessly reinvented.
*Refs:* **The Crew** (co-op), **The Fox in the Forest** (2p), **Tichu**, **Skat**.

### Wargames (hex-and-counter, COIN)
Simulate conflict. **Hex-and-counter:** units on a hex grid + combat-results tables. **COIN** (GMT): asymmetric multi-faction politico-military, card-driven.
*Refs:* Hex — **Combat Commander**, **ASL**. COIN — **A Distant Plain**, **Cuba Libre**, **Twilight Struggle** (adjacent), **Root** (COIN-derived, mass-market).

### 4X (eXplore, eXpand, eXploit, eXterminate)
Empire-building across all four Xs; long and epic.
*Refs:* **Twilight Imperium 4E** (flagship), **Eclipse**, **Clash of Cultures**.

### Economic
Production, markets, supply/demand, money, price manipulation.
*Refs:* **Power Grid**, **Food Chain Magnate**, **Brass** series, **Container**.

### Party
Large groups, short rules, social/laughter, light strategy.
*Refs:* **Codenames** (SdJ 2016), **Dixit** (SdJ 2010), **Wavelength**, **Just One** (SdJ 2019).

### Abstract
Little/no theme; often perfect information, minimal luck; pure positional strategy.
*Refs:* **Chess**, **Go**, **Hive**, **Azul**, **Santorini**, **YINSH**.

### Dungeon Crawler
Heroes explore a modular dungeon, fight, loot, level up; often campaign + miniatures-heavy.
*Refs:* **Gloomhaven**/**Frosthaven**, **Descent**, **Sword & Sorcery**.

## Descriptors & sub-styles

- **Euro vs Ameritrash (thematic)** — a *spectrum*, not a binary. **Euro:** indirect interaction,
  optimization, low output-luck, no elimination, VP-driven, theme "pasted on" (Agricola, Concordia,
  Brass). **Ameritrash/thematic:** strong narrative, direct conflict, dice combat, dramatic swings,
  player powers, possible elimination (Twilight Imperium, Blood Rage). Modern design increasingly
  **hybridizes** ("Euro-thematic": Scythe, Ark Nova, Root).
- **Point Salad** — many parallel, independent scoring avenues so almost any action yields VP and
  multiple strategies coexist (Terraforming Mars, Ark Nova, Castles of Burgundy).
- **Tableau Building** — build a personal array of cards granting ongoing powers/combos (Race for the
  Galaxy, 7 Wonders, Everdell, Wingspan).
- **Bag Building** — deck-building's cousin: draw tokens/chips blindly from a bag you improve (adds
  output randomness + push-your-luck) (Quacks, Orléans, Automobiles).

## How games blend mechanisms

Good design combines familiar mechanisms so each covers the other's weakness, or to create a novel
decision. **A good blend gives players a reason each mechanism *needs* the other; a weak blend just
stacks systems (bloat).** Concrete examples:

- **Dune: Imperium = deck-building + worker placement.** Your hand determines which worker spaces you
  can use; leftover cards resolve in combat. Fixes deck-building's lack of board interaction; gives
  worker placement input variety.
- **Lost Ruins of Arnak = deck-building + worker placement + exploration.** Deck powers fuel movement;
  new sites unlock a research track. Exploration gives the engine a spatial goal.
- **Everdell = worker placement + tableau engine.** Workers gather; resources build a comboing tableau.
  Satisfying build-up arc + tactile theme + light action-denial.
- **Ark Nova = card-driven tableau + action selection + hex area.** An action-card row (rondel-like
  priority) gates actions; you build a zoo and chase many scoring tracks. Huge decision space with a
  tight action economy.
- **Wingspan = engine-building + dice drafting (birdfeeder) + set collection.** Habitat rows cascade;
  the feeder + end-of-round goals add input randomness and direction.
- **Root = area control + deep asymmetry + hand management + COIN wargame.** The "blend" *is* the
  asymmetry — each faction is a different mechanism set on one shared map.
- **Scythe = engine building + area presence + resource management + light combat.** Combat rarely
  fires; the *threat* shapes an otherwise Euro optimization game.
- **Quacks = bag building + push-your-luck.** Bag improvement is the engine; the draw is the gamble.
- **Clank! = deck-building + push-your-luck + dungeon movement.** The deck moves you deeper as risk
  escalates.
- **Gloomhaven = dungeon crawl + hand management (dual-use cards) + legacy campaign.** No-dice
  card-selection combat is the signature.
