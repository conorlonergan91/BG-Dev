# Representation & Sensitivity

A **low-probability, high-consequence** quality lever. Most elegant 2-player games never touch it; but when
a game draws its theme, setting, or art from **real cultures, histories, peoples, or lived experience**, a
misstep becomes *the thing the game is remembered for* — and it's usually invisible to the people who made
it. This is a light guardian: cheap to run, occasionally decisive. Owned jointly by **`bg-theme`** (theme &
art — where representation lives) and **`bg-publishing`** (reputational & contractual risk). It is
**general guidance, not a substitute for a paid sensitivity/cultural consultant** when the theme warrants
one.

## When to actually engage (the trigger)
Run a sensitivity pass **only when the theme touches real-world people or cultures**. Triggers:
- A setting rooted in a **real culture, region, religion, or historical period** (esp. colonial,
  indigenous, wartime, or marginalised-people themes).
- **Human depictions** in art — who's shown, how, doing what, and who's *absent*.
- Mechanics that **model real oppression or trauma** (slavery, conquest, colonisation, war) as neutral
  "engine" fodder — the classic "make X into a resource-optimisation puzzle" misfire.
- Names, iconography, language, or motifs borrowed from a living culture.

A purely abstract or invented-world game (much of this studio's elegant duel/co-op space) usually **passes
trivially** — note it and move on. Don't manufacture concerns where the theme is a lighthouse and some boats.

## The checklist (when triggered)
- **Whose story is it, and who's telling it?** Are the people depicted the *subject* (agency, dignity) or
  the *scenery/obstacle*? Is a real culture reduced to a stereotype, a costume, or an "exotic" flavour?
- **What does the mechanic *say*?** Mechanics carry meaning (MDA). Making conquest/colonisation the
  winning strategy, or a real people the resource being spent, editorialises whether you meant to. Decide
  deliberately; theme and mechanic should agree on the message.
- **Representation & absence** — is the cast one-note (default-everything)? Absence is a choice too. Cozy/
  broad-audience games especially benefit from art that lets more players see themselves at the table
  (cross-ref `accessibility.md` → socioeconomic/inclusion).
- **Borrowing vs. appropriation** — sacred symbols, languages, and living traditions used as decoration are
  the sharp edge. When in doubt, invent, abstract, or **bring in a consultant from that culture**.
- **Get the right eyes on it** — for anything genuinely rooted in a real culture, a **paid sensitivity
  reader / cultural consultant from that community** *before print* is cheap insurance against a
  reputationally catastrophic, unfixable-after-print mistake. Tabletop-specific consultants exist
  (re-verify current names live).

## How it plugs into the studio
- **`bg-theme`** runs the trigger check whenever it frames or re-themes identity, and owns the depiction/
  representation call in art briefs. A clean pass is logged and forgotten; a real flag goes to the human.
- **`bg-publishing`** carries the *risk* framing (reputational, and contractual if a consultant/contributor
  is engaged) and flags when a paid consultant is warranted.
- Cross-refs: `theme-and-identity.md` (identity & art), `accessibility.md` (inclusion/socioeconomic),
  `publishing-ip.md` (contributor contracts).

> **General information, not legal or cultural advice.** For a theme rooted in a specific real culture,
