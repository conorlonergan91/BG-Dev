# Publishing, IP & Compliance — Ireland / UK / USA

> **General information, not legal advice.** IP and product-safety law is fact-specific and changes.
> Before spending on registrations, signing contracts, or shipping physical product, confirm with a
> qualified solicitor/attorney and (for safety) an accredited test lab in each market.

## 1. What is and isn't protectable

**Copyright protects *expression*, never the underlying *idea, system, method, or rules*.**

**NOT protectable by copyright:**
- **Game mechanics / systems / procedures** (worker placement, deck-building, etc.). Anyone can build a
  different game on the same mechanics.
- The **idea/concept/theme** ("a game about running a vineyard").
- The **name/title** itself (→ trademark instead).
- **Bare functional rules.** *Idea–expression dichotomy* + **merger doctrine**: where a rule can only
  be expressed one/few ways, protection is thin or nil.

**IS protectable by copyright:**
- **Artwork and illustrations** (box, card, board art).
- **Graphic design / layout / iconography.**
- **Rulebook *text*** (creative prose, examples, flavour) as a **literary work** — copying the wording
  infringes; copying the rules themselves generally doesn't.
- **Flavour text, story, character/world descriptions.**

**Other regimes:** name/logo → **trademark**; overall box look ("trade dress") → trademark / passing-off;
appearance of physical components → **registered design rights**.

**Takeaway:** you can't stop a competitor copying your *mechanics*; you *can* stop them copying your
art, rulebook wording, name/logo, and distinctive get-up. **Put your protectable value in distinctive
art, writing, branding, and trade dress.**

## 2. Copyright by country (+ Berne)

Under the **Berne Convention** (IE, UK, US all members) copyright is **automatic on fixation** — no
registration, © notice, or fee required to *have* it, and a foreign author is protected as a domestic one.

- **Ireland — Copyright and Related Rights Act 2000.** Automatic; **no registration system exists**.
  Keep dated originals as evidence.
- **UK — Copyright, Designs and Patents Act 1988 (CDPA).** Automatic; **no registration**. Introduced
  **moral rights** (be identified as author; object to derogatory treatment) — relevant when
  commissioning artists.
- **USA — registration matters a lot for enforcement:**
  - You **cannot file an infringement suit for a US work until the Copyright Office acts** on your
    application (*Fourth Estate v. Wall-Street.com*, 2019). Processing takes months; paid expedite
    exists.
  - **Statutory damages + attorney's fees only if registered *before* infringement began, or within
    3 months of first publication** (§412). Otherwise you're limited to hard-to-prove actual damages.
  - **Practical:** register the **rulebook + artwork** at/near publication (within the 3-month window).
    Cheap relative to the leverage.

## 3. Trademark — the game name & logo (usually an indie's most valuable registrable right)

Territorial; registered per **class**: board games = **class 28**; digital/app = **9**; entertainment
services = **41**. Do a **free clearance/knockout search** first (IPOI/EUIPO/UKIPO/USPTO databases +
BGG + app stores + domains/socials) *before* committing to a name.

| Office | Territory | Rough official fee (1st class) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **IPOI** | Ireland only | ~€247 (€70 app + €177 reg); +€70/extra class | Cheapest if IE-only |
| **EUIPO** | All 27 EU states (incl. IE) | ~€850 (+€50 2nd, +€150 each beyond) | One filing covers the EU — usually better value if selling EU-wide |
| **UKIPO** | UK | ~£205 (+£60/extra class); fees rising ~25% from 1 Apr 2026 | |
| **USPTO** | USA | ~$350/class (base) | Requires use/intent-to-use; maintenance filings |

- **Madrid Protocol (WIPO):** one international application from a home registration, designating
  multiple countries — worth it once targeting several territories (roughly $1,050–$1,500 for ~3
  countries/2 classes vs $2,500–$7,500 filing nationally).
- Unregistered rights exist (passing-off IE/UK, common-law US) but are harder/costlier to enforce.
- **Sequencing for a first-timer:** clear the name → register at least home-country/EU class 28 →
  *then* spend on art/printing → add US/others via Madrid at launch.

## 4. Patents & registered design rights

- **Patents — almost never right.** EU/UK/IE **expressly exclude "rules/methods for playing games"**
  from patentability. In the US a genuinely novel mechanism *can* be patented but it's slow, expensive,
  and public — not worth it for the vast majority of tabletop games.
- **Registered design rights — the practical option for *appearance*.** Protect the visual look of a
  product/component/packaging. **UK Registered Design** and **EU Registered Community Design (EUIPO,
  covers IE)** last up to 25 years. Must be **new — file before any public disclosure** (reveal can
  destroy novelty). Unregistered design right is a weaker automatic backstop. Consider only for a
  genuinely distinctive visual/physical element; otherwise artwork copyright + trade dress usually
  cover the look.

## 5. Practical pitfalls

- **Clone risk (both ways).** Reimplementing mechanics is legal; copying **art, rulebook wording,
  layouts, iconography, or distinctive box get-up** is infringement. Re-express everything in your own
  art/words. You can't stop others cloning *your* mechanics — protect brand & presentation.
- **Other games' terms / licensed IP.** Trademarked terms, named systems, licensed themes (films,
  sports, brands), and real people's names/likenesses need permission. Generic mechanic descriptions
  are fine; brand names are not.
- **Fonts:** the font *file* is usually licensed — confirm commercial/embedding/print use. "Free for
  personal use" ≠ commercial.
- **Stock / CC / AI art:** read the licence (CC-NC = no commercial; ND; attribution). "Royalty-free"
  still has terms. **Public domain** is genuinely free but verify the specific scan/edition isn't
  separately claimed. **AI-generated art** has contested/limited US copyright (the US Copyright Office
  has refused purely AI images lacking human authorship) — a real concern if you rely on registration,
  and increasingly a backer-trust issue.
- **Contributor contracts — get assignments in writing.** Defaults differ:
  - **US:** "work made for hire" covers employees, or contractors only if the work fits a statutory
    category **and** there's a signed WFH agreement. Safer: **written contract with express copyright
    assignment**.
  - **UK & IE (and EU):** **no automatic work-for-hire for freelancers — the freelancer owns copyright
    by default even though you paid them**, unless the contract **expressly assigns it in writing**.
    Address **moral rights** too.
  - Every artist/designer/editor/sculptor signs a contract that **assigns copyright (or grants the exact
    licence you need)**, covers moral rights, and warrants originality — **before** launch.
- **Route to market:** self-publish (control + margin, all risk/logistics/compliance) vs licence/pitch
  (publisher funds & distributes, ~3–7% designer royalty, expect rejection, cede control) vs hybrid
  (crowdfund a small run to prove demand, then licence).

## 6. Physical-product safety compliance (mandatory if self-publishing a toy/game for under-14s)

A game is generally a **toy** if designed/intended for play by children under 14 (most family/kids
games qualify; strictly-adult games may fall outside toy rules but still face general product-safety
law). As the manufacturer/importer placing it on the market you must comply:

- **EU / Ireland:** **Toy Safety Directive 2009/48/EC + EN 71** standards; lab testing; draw up a
  **Declaration of Conformity**; keep technical documentation; **affix the CE mark** (self-declaration
  backed by testing).
- **UK:** **CE mark accepted indefinitely** for toys (Product Safety and Metrology (Amendment)
  Regulations 2024, in force 1 Oct 2024) — a CE-marked toy meeting EU standards can be sold in GB
  without a separate UKCA mark. NI follows EU/CE rules. Requirements mirror EN 71.
- **USA:** **ASTM F963** (mandatory toy standard) under **CPSIA**; third-party testing at a
  CPSC-accepted lab; issue a **Children's Product Certificate (CPC)**; add a **CPSIA tracking label**
  (manufacturer, location, batch/date). Lead/phthalate limits apply.
- **Choking-hazard / small-parts warnings** required on both continents. US (ASTM F963) mandates the
  exact **"CHOKING HAZARD — Small parts. Not for children under 3 yrs."** wording with prescribed
  size/placement (front panel). EU/EN 71 requires the small-parts warning for toys aimed under 36 months.
- **Age grading** on packaging.
- **Barcode:** GS1 EAN/UPC if selling via retail/distribution (see `manufacturing-specs.md`).

## 7. Indie IP & compliance checklist (IE / UK / US)

**Clearance (before spending on art/printing)**
- [ ] Accept mechanics aren't protectable — build value in art, writing, brand, trade dress.
- [ ] Name chosen + **free trademark clearance search** (IPOI/EUIPO/UKIPO/USPTO + BGG + app stores +
      domain/socials).
- [ ] Not copying another game's art/wording/layout/iconography/get-up; no unlicensed trademarked terms.

**Copyright**
- [ ] IE/UK: nothing to file — keep dated originals/working files as evidence.
- [ ] US: **register rulebook + artwork at/near publication (within 3 months)**.
- [ ] Verify licences for **every font, image, asset**; keep records. Be cautious relying on
      unregisterable AI art.

**Contracts (before launch)**
- [ ] Every contributor signs a written **copyright assignment / defined licence** (UK/IE freelancers
      own by default; US shouldn't rely on WFH alone); address moral rights + originality warranty.

**Trademark**
- [ ] Register name/logo in **class 28** (and 9/41 if relevant): IPOI (IE-only) or **EUIPO** (EU-wide),
      UKIPO (UK), USPTO (US); use **Madrid** once multi-territory.

**Design rights (optional)**
- [ ] Consider a UK/EU **registered design** for a genuinely distinctive element — **file before public
      reveal**. Skip patents (EU/UK exclude game rules; US rarely worth it).

**Physical compliance (mandatory if self-publishing a toy)**
- [ ] EU/IE: Toy Safety Directive + **EN 71**, testing, **Declaration of Conformity**, technical file,
      **CE mark**.
- [ ] UK: CE accepted (or UKCA); equivalent EN 71.
- [ ] US: **ASTM F963 / CPSIA**, CPSC-lab testing, **CPC**, **tracking label**.
- [ ] Correct **age grading** + **choking/small-parts warnings** (US wording is prescriptive).
- [ ] **GS1 EAN/UPC** if retail/distribution.

*Sources: IPOI, UKIPO, EUIPO, USPTO, US Copyright Office, Copyright Alliance, Toy Safety Directive /
EN 71, CPSC/ASTM F963, GS1 — all to be re-verified live for a real launch.*
