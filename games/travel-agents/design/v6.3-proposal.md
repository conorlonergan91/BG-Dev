## Change 1 — Reputation given teeth (the big one)

### The problem (CD's verdict)
Reputation is currently **pure end-game VP by track position** (3→+2 · 6→+4 · 9→+6 · top→+9), earned as a
side-effect of claiming vendors (+1 drink/daytime, +2/+3 night/apex). It has **no in-game consequence** —
you never make a decision *because* of your Rep during play. It's a passive accumulator that gets totalled
once. Dead weight.

### New direction (CD)
**Reputation gates Buzz** — you need Rep to place Buzz — and you **earn Rep by TRAVELLING** (spreading the
word) and through **some events**. This gives Rep a live economic job and re-homes it as the fuel for the
signature twist.

### The current Buzz economy (what we're gating)
Buzz is **not** held in hand — **Promote** places Buzz *directly from supply onto co-located vendors*
(2 per Promote; 網紅 places 3). To **Bribe** a vendor you must hold a **≥2 Buzz majority** on it over your
rival (大哥: ≥1). Buzz lives publicly *on vendors* and clears to supply when the vendor is won. So the gate
has to act on **how much Buzz you can commit**, not on a Buzz hand.

### The three shapes

**Option 1A — Rep as a SPEND currency ("Buzz costs Rep").**
Each Buzz you place costs 1 Rep (Promote = pay 2 Rep, place 2 Buzz). Rep is a consumable pool that Travel
refills.
- *Pros:* dead simple to state; makes Buzz genuinely scarce.
- *Cons:* 1:1 spend makes Rep feel like nothing more than **pre-bought Buzz** — thematically thin, and it
  begs the question "why two things?". Worse for the design: scarcer Buzz means players contest **less**,
  which pushes the already-rare Buzz fight (Risk #1, ~5% contested) *rarer* — the wrong direction.

**Option 1B — Rep as a live CAP on committed Buzz ("your hype can't outrun your reputation"). ★ RECOMMENDED**
One rule: **the total Buzz you have committed across the board may never exceed your current Reputation.**
Rep is a persistent capability level (a marker climbing the existing Rep track), not consumed. Winning a
vendor clears your Buzz off it → frees your cap again. Travel raises the cap.
- *Pros:*
  - **One sentence, no per-token payment, no new component** (the Rep track already exists; it just changes
    from a scoring track to a resource track).
  - **Makes the Buzz fight RICHER, not rarer.** It adds a resource dimension to *who wins a collision*: to
    out-hype a rival by ≥2 on a vendor you need Rep to cover the whole stack, so **the player who travelled
    more can out-escalate**. "I can't win this fight — my Rep's too low — I need to get around and build it
    first" is a real, legible decision every turn.
  - **Emergent focus → more collisions.** Because a global cap punishes spreading Buzz thin across many
    vendors, players are nudged to **commit deep on fewer vendors** — which makes agents *converge* on the
    same prizes more often. That directly attacks Risk #1's rarity from a second angle.
  - **Travel becomes ammunition.** Travel is currently the weakest action (move + draw, event whiffs). Now
    it fuels your whole Buzz economy → it earns its turn *and* it pulls agents around the map, helping the
    Risk #3 spread problem (Central/East under-pull).
- *Cons:* a little table-tracking ("is my committed Buzz ≤ my Rep?"). At 2p you have ~3–6 tokens out and a
  marker on a track — countable at a glance. If it feels fiddly at the table, fall back to the per-turn
  variant (below).

**Option 1C — Hybrid (floor gate + per-Promote cap).**
Need Rep ≥ 1 to Promote at all; each Promote may place Buzz up to your Rep (a per-*action* cap, not a
global committed cap).
- *Pros:* no "count all my board Buzz" step — you only check Rep at the moment you Promote.
- *Cons:* loses the emergent *focus→collision* property that makes 1B good for Risk #1; two clauses instead
  of one. This is the **lighter-tracking fallback** if 1B's global count annoys testers — keep it in
  reserve rather than shipping it first.

> **Recommendation: 1B (Rep = a live cap on committed Buzz), with 1C held in reserve as the
> lighter-tracking dial.** It is the only shape that makes the signature fight *deeper AND more frequent*
> while turning the dud Travel action into the engine's fuel line — all from a single rule and zero new
> components. Starting Rep **2** (you can run one small campaign from turn 1; escalation requires travel).

### Sub-decision 1-i — How is Rep EARNED? (and do vendors still grant it?)
Rep must have a healthy source now that it's fuel. The danger is a **flywheel**: if claiming a vendor grants
Rep, then *claim → more Rep → more Buzz → more claims* is rich-get-runaway. Travel-as-source is the CD's
instinct and it's the right one — it decouples the flywheel and is thematically perfect (a promoter builds
their name by *getting around and spreading the word*).

- **Option i-A — Travel + events only; vendors grant NO Rep. ★ RECOMMENDED.** `Travel = +1 Rep`; ~3–4
  event cards across the ingredient decks grant +1 Rep ("word spreads"). Vendors drop their Rep value
  entirely. *Wins:* breaks the flywheel; removes the Rep number from **every vendor card** (parseability +
  keyword-ceiling win); makes Travel a first-class action. *Watch:* Rep must not choke — size Travel income
  and the event count so the Buzz economy breathes (a sim tuning knob).
- **Option i-B — Travel + events, plus DRINKS grant +1 Rep.** Re-homes drinks' weak value: a cheap popular
  drink stand "spreads your name." Gives drinks a real second job (helps the "drinks are a viable filler"
  goal). *Cost:* a mild flywheel returns via drinks, and drinks keep a Rep number.
- **Option i-C — Keep vendor Rep grants as-is.** Not recommended — this is the flywheel the whole redesign
  should avoid, and it keeps a now-confusing number on every card.

> **Recommendation: i-A.** Travel `+1` Rep, ~3–4 events `+1` Rep, vendors grant none. Exact numbers are a
> **sim tuning knob** — the risk is *undershooting* (Rep too scarce → fight chokes), so start generous and
> tighten. (If the table finds drinks limp, i-B is the cheap patch.)

### Sub-decision 1-ii — Does Rep still convert to end-VP?
- **Option ii-A — NO. Gating Buzz REPLACES Rep's scoring role. ★ RECOMMENDED.** Once Rep has a live
  economic job, letting it *also* score is double-dipping — and because Rep now comes from Travel, scoring
  it would over-r:eward travel-spam and risk a dominant lane (bad for the five-playstyle balance). Removing
  the end-VP conversion also **deletes the `3→+2·6→+4·9→+6·top→+9` scoring table** — a clean elegance win
  (one fewer scoring subsystem, less rulebook prose against the over-cap flag). The Rep *track* stays on the
  board; it just stops converting to points.
- **Option ii-B — YES, keep a reduced end-VP conversion** (e.g. only the `top→+3` tier). Preserves a third
  scoring vector but reintroduces the double-dip tension and keeps a scoring line.

> **Recommendation: ii-A (Rep no longer scores).** Its job is now to *win you vendors and tickets*, which
> is where the points already live. Prestige (Bookings) + Tickets remain the two scoring vectors; the five
> playstyles are served by those + the contest layer, not by a passive Rep total.

> **Knock-on — tiebreak.** The current tiebreak is "highest Reputation, then most vendors, then most
> regions." With Rep now a fluctuating *spent/gated* resource, ranking on it is perverse (a player who
> poured Rep into a winning hype campaign would be penalised). **New tiebreak: most vendors → most regions
> covered → fewest tickets in the Ones-That-Got-Away pile.** (Flagged as an open sub-decision below.)

### Knock-ons the CD asked me to address
- **(i) Double-dip / scoring** — handled above: **replace**, don't add. Removing Rep-VP is a net simplify.
- **(ii) The Buzz fight (Risk #1)** — **1B makes the fight RICHER, not rarer.** It doesn't change collision
  *geometry* (that's board spread + the contest tickets 搶手貨/人氣王 + Change 2's milestone lure), but it
  adds a travel-earned resource layer to *who wins* a fight and — via the global-cap focus incentive —
  nudges agents to converge. Net: the fight gets a strategic spine it currently lacks. This is exactly the
  kind of depth Risk #1 wanted.
- **(iii) Kit interactions:**
  - **網紅 (Promote places 3):** under a Rep cap, 網紅 still places 3 in one action but is bounded by Rep
    like everyone — early game its 3 is throttled to its Rep (e.g. capped at 2 at start). This is a
    **natural, welcome soft-nerf** on the character the sim flagged as over-strong (55%), and it may
    partially substitute for the fragile "Late to the Party" lost-turn flaw. **Watch, don't pre-tweak:** let
    the re-sim tell us whether 網紅 still needs the lost-turn flaw or whether the Rep gate does the work.
  - **大哥 (Bribe at ≥1 margin):** 大哥 needs *less* Buzz to win a fight → needs *less* Rep → is *less*
    travel-dependent than everyone else in the new economy. That's a **relative buff** (大哥 was already
    51.7%). **Flag for the re-sim** — 大哥 may drift up; the dial if so is to restore a small flaw, not to
    touch the Rep rule.
  - **里長伯 / 媽寶 / 大胃王:** no interaction issues; 里長伯's discount and 媽寶's/大胃王's sourcing kits are
    orthogonal to the Buzz gate.
  - **Net:** **no kit rewrite proposed for v6.3** — the Rep gate reshapes the field and the re-sim should
    *re-read* all five before we touch a mat.

> ### ⚠ RE-SIM REQUIRED (gated on CD sign-off)
> Change 1 is **NOT sim-equivalent.** It rewrites the Buzz economy (source, cap, and the fight's resolution
> dimension), removes a scoring vector, and shifts the character field. The v6.2 sim's win-rates do **not**
> carry over. This needs a fresh run — **and per studio protocol the sim is gated on the CD's explicit
> "go."** Changes 2 and 3 fold into the same run. Key sim probes to add: Rep-income sizing (does the fight
> choke or breathe?), contested-vendor rate vs v6.2's ~5% (does the focus incentive lift it?), the five
> playstyle band, 大哥 drift, and whether 網紅 still needs its lost-turn flaw.

## Change 2 — Milestone boons on the Bookings track

Add **~2 milestone marks** on the Bookings (VP) track; the **first player to cross each** gets a **one-time
boon**. Constraint (bg-elegance flags we're near the keyword/component ceiling): boons must **reuse existing
currencies** — no new subsystem.

### Which way does it cut? (catch-up vs runaway — the honest read)
"First to cross" is inherently a **leader-reward**, so left naive it tilts **runaway** (the player already
ahead scoops the boon and pulls further ahead). Three mitigations turn it into a healthy **race incentive**
instead:
1. **Small, NON-VP boons only.** A boon that hands *points* (free Bookings, or ★ Stars that become points)
   directly extends the lead = pure runaway. A boon that hands *engine fuel* (a Rep bump, a free ingredient,
   a discount) helps you *keep pace* and is **worth more to a trailing player** than a runaway one.
2. **Place the marks EARLY/MID, not late.** A milestone near the finish just crowns the winner. Marks at
   ~1/3 and ~2/3 of the track are reached while the game is still open, so *either* player can win the race
   — that's the tension you want.
3. **One-time, non-compounding.** Each mark fires once, ever.

With those three, this cuts as a **race incentive with a mild runaway tilt** — acceptable, and it does a
job the game wants: it gives players a *reason to push Bookings tempo* and a shared target to converge on
(a second nudge toward the Buzz fight, since both may sprint for the same vendors to hit a mark first).

### Mark positions
Bookings ≈ live Prestige (Rep no longer scores; tickets settle at game end), running ~0 → ~20–24 at 2p.
Propose marks at **Bookings 7 and 14** (~1/3 and ~2/3). Tunable — flag as a sim/table knob; the risk is
placing them so late they only reward the leader.

### Candidate boon sets (all reuse existing currencies)

**Set A — "Tempo & Fuel." ★ RECOMMENDED.**
- **Mark 7 → +1 Reputation** (raises your Buzz cap — see Change 1; pure fight-fuel, no points).
- **Mark 14 → take 1 ingredient of your choice + your next Bribe costs 1 less.**
- *Why:* all non-VP tempo; the Rep bump is **only meaningful because of Change 1**, so the two changes
  reinforce each other; a trailing player values the fuel more than a leader does → dampens runaway.

**Set B — "Star-maker."**
- **Mark 7 → take a ★ Star token** (place on any vendor: +2 Bookings/+1 Rep, recyclable).
- **Mark 14 → a second ★ Star token.**
- *Why/why-not:* thematic (you're literally making a stall famous) and it lures a fight onto the starred
  vendor. But ★ funnels toward *points* → more runaway, and ★ tokens are a limited supply (~4) so this
  competes with the existing Star economy.

**Set C — "Minimal footprint."**
- **Both marks → +1 Rep and draw 1 ingredient** (identical boon at each).
- *Why:* smallest possible rules text (one boon to teach). Least interesting, but the safest budget play if
  bg-elegance rules that two distinct boons is a keyword the ceiling can't afford.

> **Recommendation: Set A**, marks at **7 / 14**. It reuses Rep (now live), ingredients, and the
> Bribe-discount already in the game; adds no keyword beyond "milestone" (one board icon + one line); and
> the Rep-bump boon compounds the *good* part of Change 1. If bg-elegance says even two distinct boons is
> over the line, collapse to **Set C**.

> **Balance status:** adds resources → **not sim-neutral; rides the Change-1 re-sim.** Low-risk relative to
> Change 1. Sim probe: does the leader hoover both marks (runaway) or do they genuinely change hands?

## Change 3 — Perks simplified to three types

Every vendor perk must collapse to exactly one of: **{Bookings-only (flat bonus points) · engine-discount-
only (region-icon ramp) · both}**. Remap the v6.2 perk vocabulary onto this taxonomy and re-issue the table.

### Re-mapping the v6.2 perk vocabulary
The v6.2 spread uses four perk sub-terms. Here's where each lands — and note that **three of the four die**:
| v6.2 perk term | v6.3 disposition |
|---|---|
| `icon N/C/S/E` (single region discount) | → **engine-discount** (the ramp — kept intact, it's the engine spine) |
| `double-icon N` | **DIES.** Magnitude folds into base Bookings; the vendor becomes a normal **1-icon** card, extra value expressed as points. No more "double" special case. |
| `wild icon` | **DIES.** Becomes the card's **own-region icon** (or a plain "both"). No more wild-flex special case. |
| `+2 Rep / court` (apex) | **DIES** — forced by Change 1 (Rep is no longer a claim reward). Apex trophies become **Bookings-only** (their value is just high points). |

### The deeper elegance win (recommended framing)
Once `double-icon`, `wild`, and `+Rep/court` are gone, the "perk" is no longer a distinct card element at
all — **it collapses into two attributes the card already carries:**
- **Bookings value** (points), and
- **an icon, or no icon** (ramp, or not).

The CD's "three types" are then just the three meaningful *combinations* of those two attributes:
- **discount-only** = has an icon, ordinary points → *engine piece* (all daytime food).
- **both** = has an icon AND high points → *premium engine piece* (mid-night food).
- **Bookings-only** = no icon, high points → *trophy* (the apex ★).
- (**drinks** = low points, no icon = the no-engine floor.)

So the recommendation is **stronger than "keep a perk field with 3 legal values": eliminate the perk field
entirely** — a vendor card is `Bookings value + (icon | no icon)`. That's a real keyword/parseability win
(bg-infodesign gets a simpler card; bg-elegance gets keywords back under the 15 ceiling). The CD's literal
"perk field with 3 allowed values" is the conservative fallback if we ever want a perk that *isn't*
expressible as points+icon — but nothing in the current spread needs it.

## Change 4 — Ticket pools 3 → 2 (DAY and NIGHT)

Collapse **daytime / nighttime / general** into just **two decks: DAY and NIGHT.** The old general/"any"
tickets distribute into both decks; their *contents* aren't day/night-specific, and **the card's own text
(equivalently: which deck it sits in) states when it scores** — DAY tickets judged **at dusk**, NIGHT
tickets **at the last train**.

### The clean-up this delivers
v6.2 carried a special-case rule: *"a general-pool ticket is judged at the end of whichever phase's row it
was claimed from."* Collapsing to two decks **deletes that branch** — a ticket's deck now *is* its judging
trigger (DAY→dusk, NIGHT→last train). One fewer rule; the win/lose-pile timing reconciles automatically.
**Elegance win.**

### Distributing the 3 general tickets (by feasibility, not by fiat)
The general patterns aren't day/night-flavoured, but each must land where it's *achievable within that
phase's clock* (dusk falls at the 5th table-wide claim @2p — very few claims happen before dusk):
| General ticket | Lands in | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **遍地開花** vendor in 3 regions | **both** (one copy each) | achievable either phase; the DAY copy scores at dusk, the NIGHT copy at the last train |
| **收藏家** 5 vendors total | **NIGHT** | ~5 total claims table-wide before dusk makes "5 of your own" impossible by dusk |
| **揮金如土** ≥12 Bookings | **NIGHT** | needs accumulation only the night half affords |

### Resulting deck sizes & the draw model
- **DAY deck ≈ 13** (12 daytime + 1 general copy: 遍地開花).
- **NIGHT deck ≈ 14** (12 nighttime + 2 general: 收藏家, 揮金如土, + the NIGHT 遍地開花 copy → ~15; trim one
  overlapping night pattern to hold ~14 for symmetry).
- **The 5-draw-per-phase model still works unchanged** — you still draw 5 from that phase's single deck; the
  face-up row stays **3**, refilled on claim. The only change is *which pile a pattern can appear in*, which
  is now decided at setup rather than by a runtime "claimed-from-which-row" rule.

> **Recommendation: adopt as above.** Two decks, general folded by feasibility, 遍地開花 duplicated across
> both. Final per-deck counts (13/14) are a **light tuning knob** — keep them within ~1 of each other so
> neither phase's 5-draw is thinner.
>
> **Balance status: SIM-NEUTRAL.** This is a setup/handling change — same patterns, same values, same
> win/lose scoring. The v6.2 sim didn't model the general pool as a distinct third distribution anyway.
> **No re-sim needed for balance.**

## Change 5 — Per-player win/lose piles (confirmation)

**Confirmed rule: each player has their OWN two piles — a WIN pile (成團 *Published Tours*) and a LOSE pile
(遺珠 *the Ones That Got Away*) — entirely independent of any opponent's.** A player's ticket outcomes and
final tally never touch another player's piles.

This is already how v6.2 reads ("sorted into **your** WIN pile / LOSE pile"; "sits in front of **you** as an
open commitment"), so v6.3 simply makes it explicit and unambiguous for the rulebook. The physical
relocation of the two piles onto the character mats is **bg-infodesign's** call — this doc only owns the
*rule* that they're per-player.

> **Balance status: SIM-NEUTRAL** (a confirmation, no mechanical change).
