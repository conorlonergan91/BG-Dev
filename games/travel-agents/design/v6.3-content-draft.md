### CD-2 — NEW EVENT "Scheduled Maintenance" (排班保養) — re-homes the no-HSR concept as TABLE-WIDE

The **HSR (North↔South shortcut) goes down for ALL players** — everyone takes the slow road around the ring
(N→C→S or N→E→S, **two** Travels instead of the one HSR hop) for the next round of turns. Warm/secular/
comedic: *"the high-speed rail's down for scheduled maintenance — slow road for everyone, you included."*

**Scope handling — the clean call: a SPECIAL TABLE-WIDE event, NOT a new ⦿/⬢/➤ glyph.** The ⦿/⬢/➤ scopes
all share one semantic — *measured from the drawn region, hits whoever stands where, resolves instantly,
then discards.* Scheduled Maintenance breaks all three: it is **not region-relative** (it disables a global
board feature), **not location-keyed** (it hits everyone regardless of where they stand), and it
**PERSISTS** (a duration, not an instant resolve). Overloading a 4th "island-wide" glyph into that family
would falsely imply it shares those properties and muddy the clean "read the scope from the drawn region"
teach. So it is handled as **one distinctly-framed table-wide event card** (a full-width "island-wide"
banner frame, visually obvious that it is not a ⦿/⬢/➤ card). **Restraint:** we do NOT formalise a whole
"island-wide scope" taxonomy for a single card — if more persistent-global events ever arrive, formalise it
then. *(Flag for bg-elegance: this is a new event **sub-type**, not a new keyword; and for bg-infodesign:
it needs its own banner frame + an HSR-closed reminder marker on the board's HSR spine.)*

- **Structural rule (duration):** when drawn, place it face-up on the HSR spine as a reminder. The HSR link
  is **closed for all players until the start of the drawing player's next turn** (one full go-around);
  while closed, North and South are **not adjacent** — Travel between them takes the long way. Then the
  card is discarded and the HSR reopens.
- **Fires like any event** — on a **Visit** or **Convenience Store** flip (not on a Travel arrival, per
  §6). Because a Convenience-Store flip reaches any region, it can be triggered from anywhere.
- **Where it lives (re-sim knob):** seed **1 copy** in a **NIGHT** ingredient deck of an **HSR-endpoint
  region (North or South)** — it bites hardest when cross-region night sourcing matters most and matches
  the event-heavier night. Count (1 vs 2) and day-vs-night placement are knobs.
- **bg-theme names it** (working 排班保養 / "Scheduled Maintenance"); **flag for the theme sweep** — keep it
  warm/secular/comedic, a head-office-groans-on-the-phone inconvenience, never peril.

### CD-4 — NIGHT-COST ESCALATION (the LOTR: Duel model — the key balance direction)

**The concern:** in the night half a strong region-discount engine could Bribe the big night vendors "for
free" — trivialising sourcing and removing the need to Travel/Visit/pull ingredients. **This is a real
zero-out:** the discount ramp is *(number of that region's icons you hold)* off that region's ingredient
cost, floored at 0 (§5). A night apex costing `{N:3}` held against 3 North icons → `{N:0}` = free.

**The fix (shape, not final numbers):** night vendors cost **meaningfully MORE** than day vendors —
escalated enough that even a ramped engine must **still source/travel to afford the night marquee** (the
discount *defrays*, it does not *zero out*). The model is LOTR: Duel's later ages — new, pricier cards you
build your engine *toward*.

- **The deliberate cost curve — DAY = cheap/ramp-building · NIGHT = expensive/climax:**
  - **DAY vendors** stay cheap (~1 ingredient — the discount-only foods that ARE the region icons; you
    claim several to build the ramp). Held ~as printed.
  - **NIGHT "both" (premium engine) foods** step up from ~2 → **~3–4 ingredients**, biased toward including
    a **cross-region (→X)** component so a single-region ramp can't cover the whole bill.
  - **NIGHT apex ★ trophies** step up from ~3 → **~5–6 ingredients**, preferably cross-region (e.g. a
    `{N:4, S:1}` shape rather than a flat `{N:5}`).
- **The load-bearing design principle (state it, tune the numbers):** *the night marquee's cost must EXCEED
  the maximum single-region ramp a player can plausibly hold by night,* so the discount can never zero it.
  If a plausible max night ramp in a region ≈ R (say 3), a night apex's single-region portion should be
  ≥ R + ~2 (leaving ≥~2 ingredients to source), and cross-region portions force sourcing the ramp can't
  defray at all. Calibrate to **(typical night ramp) + (a mandatory residual of ~2+ ingredients).**
- **Independent of the Buzz gate:** Buzz is never discounted, so this touches only the **ingredient** gate
  (which the discount engine was trivialising). The **Reputation cap** still governs the **Buzz** gate. The
  fix restores ingredient-sourcing tension at night; the two gates stay cleanly separate.

**Re-sim knobs:** the exact per-tier cost bump (day-food / night-"both" / night-apex); the cross-region
density on night vendors; and — the open coupling below — whether the DAY ramp-build rate also needs a
light touch.

**★ THE #1 PROBE (define it precisely):** measure **sourcing actions (Visit + Convenience Store + Travel)
per turn in the NIGHT half vs the DAY half.**
- **Failure mode A (the one this fixes):** night sourcing rate **collapses toward zero** → the engine
  trivialises sourcing. Reject.
- **Goal:** night sourcing stays **meaningfully alive** — e.g. the night-half sourcing rate holds within a
  healthy fraction (~60–100%) of the day-half rate, not a collapse.
- **Failure mode B (the over-correction to watch):** night marquees become **unaffordable** → nobody Bribes
  at night, the vendor-claims clock stalls before the last-train mark, and the game drags or ends on the
  deck-exhaustion backstop. The LOTR-Duel target is **reachable-with-a-good-engine, not impossible.** Probe
  **night vendor claims/player** (the climax must still fire) and **turns-to-first-night-Bribe.**

**Two open items I flag rather than resolve on paper:**
1. **Re-cost the NIGHT tier only, or the day/night decks both?** My read: the smallest faithful change is
   **re-cost the NIGHT tier up; hold DAY as the ramp-builder.** BUT the ramp is *built from* day vendors, so
   if the day ramp overbuilds, even escalated night costs get zeroed. **Recommendation:** primary =
   escalate NIGHT; secondary/watch = the DAY ramp-build rate (touch day costs only if the sim shows the
   ramp still zeroing night). This depends on the ramp-vs-night-cost interaction the sim measures — I can't
   fully resolve it on paper.
2. **A complementary lightweight backstop — a "min-1 ingredient" floor (surfaced as an A/B/C for the
   human).** A one-line rule — *after all discounts, every Bribe still costs at least 1 ingredient of the
   vendor's cost* — structurally forbids a fully-free Bribe with **zero re-costing.** It is cheaper/lighter
   than the cost curve but a *weaker* guarantee (forces only 1 ingredient, not "meaningful" sourcing). The
   two are not exclusive. See "Decision for the human" in the return.

**Knock-on watches (all → the re-sim):**
- **Value-referencing tickets** (`d_prestige8` ≥8, `n_prestige14` ≥14, `g_prestige12` ≥12) reference *value*
  not *cost*, but pricier night vendors → fewer night claims → those thresholds get *harder*; the ticket
  values/thresholds may need a light retune alongside the cost bump (a knob).
- **Vendor-claims clock pacing:** harder night Bribes could slow the 10th-claim last-train trigger →
  confirm the game still reaches the last train (not just the deck-exhaustion backstop) and still lands
  ~25–30 min at 2p.
- **CD-1 interaction:** escalated night costs + 里長伯's away tax could over-punish him away / over-reward
  home-camping — watch his band and his region spread.
- **Rave Review Mark 2** (next Bribe −1 ingredient + a free ingredient) becomes *more* valuable under high
  night costs — a nice "the fuel lands when it's useful" synergy, not a problem.

## Change 1 — Reputation given teeth (the signature-twist redesign)

Reputation stops being a passive end-VP accumulator (the old `3→+2 · 6→+4 · 9→+6 · top→+9` table is
**DELETED**). It becomes a **live resource** that gates the signature Buzz layer. This is the one genuinely
mechanical redesign in v6.3.

### 1a. The cap rule (Option 1B — the whole rule in one line)

> **Your total Buzz committed on the board may never exceed your current Reputation.**

- **Reputation is a persistent capability level**, not a spend — a marker on the (repurposed) Rep track. It
  is **not** consumed when you Promote; it just sets a ceiling on how much Buzz you can have out at once.
- **Winning a vendor clears your Buzz off it → frees your cap** for the next campaign.
- **Starting Reputation = 2** *(sim knob)* — you can run one Promote (2 Buzz) from turn 1; escalating a
  fight requires travelling to build Reach first.
- **Reputation track runs 0–12** (the physical track from v6.2, unchanged; it just stops scoring). Rep
  **floors at 0** and **caps at the track top** *(top value a sim knob; 12 is fine — committed Buzz rarely
  approaches it, so the tension lives in the early game)*.

**Why this shape (the design win):** it makes the Buzz fight **richer *and* more frequent** without a new
component — to out-hype a rival by ≥2 on a contested vendor you need the Reputation to cover the whole
stack, so *the agent who travelled more can out-escalate*; and because a **global** cap punishes spreading
Buzz thin, players are nudged to **commit deep on fewer vendors → agents converge → more collisions**
(attacking the ~5% contested-rate concern from two angles). It also turns the dud **Travel** action into
the engine's fuel line.

### 1b. How Reputation is EARNED (sub-decision 1-i → Option i-A)

- **Travel → +1 Reputation** *(sim knob: income size; the risk is *undershooting* → the fight chokes, so
  start generous and tighten)*. Travel was the weakest action (move + draw, event whiffs); it is now
  *ammunition* — it raises your Buzz cap **and** pulls agents around the map (helping the East/Central spread
  problem).
- **~3–4 event cards → +1 Reputation** ("word spreads" boons salted through the ingredient decks) *(sim
  knob: count 3 vs 4, and magnitude)*.
- **Vendors grant NO Reputation.** The Rep value is **dropped from every vendor card** (see the reissued
  table in Change 3). This **breaks the flywheel** (claim → Rep → Buzz → claim was rich-get-runaway) and
  removes a now-confusing number from every card.

### 1c. Reputation SINKS — greed & corner-cutting cost Reputation (★ CD ADDITION)

Reputation can be **lost, not only earned.** A tight, thematic sink set deepens the push-pull: **Travel and
events build your standing; greed and cut corners burn it.** All sinks are warm-comedic (reputation lost by
shoving to the front of the queue / cutting corners), never grim.

**Voluntary sink — the Greedy Grab (one rule, two touchpoints):**
> When you source, you may **grab one extra ingredient beyond your normal limit** — each extra grab costs
> **−1 Reputation.**
- On a **Visit** (flip 3, normally keep 2): keep **+1** more (3 instead of 2; 大胃王 keeps 4 instead of 3)
  → **−1 Rep.** *(This is the CD's anchor example — a greedy grab beyond your normal draw dings your
  standing.)*
- On a **Convenience Store** (normally take 1): take the **next card too** (2 blind) → **−1 Rep.**
- *(Magnitude −1 and the "+1 extra" size are sim knobs. Whether both touchpoints ship, or just the Visit
  grab, is a light dial — the CS variant is the first thing to cut if it reads as bloat.)*

**Involuntary sinks — Rep-cost events (~2–3 cards; start with 2, sim decides on a 3rd):**
- **負評 "A Bad Review"** — scope **⦿ (the flipper)** — **−1 Reputation.** A one-star review; your reach dips.
- **出包 "A Mix-Up" ("cut corners")** — scope **⦿** — **−1 Reputation.** You cut a corner and it showed.
- *(A third Rep-hazard is a sim knob; weight these toward the **night** decks, which already run
  event-heavier. Event Rep +/− counts are a sim knob.)*

### 1d. The cap edge case — a Rep loss that would drop Rep below your committed Buzz (CD directive)

This is the one rule the Rep economy must state plainly. **Teachable in one line:**

> **Your committed Buzz can never exceed your Reputation — so a *voluntary* Rep loss you can't afford is
> simply illegal, and a *forced* (event) loss makes you pull Buzz back off the board, your choice which.**

Spelled out:
- **Voluntary sink (a Greedy Grab):** you may take it **only if it would not drop your Reputation below your
  currently-committed Buzz.** (Same as any illegal move — you just can't cut that corner right now.)
- **Forced sink (a Rep-cost event):** apply the loss; then **if your committed Buzz now exceeds your
  Reputation, immediately return Buzz from the board to the supply — you choose which — until committed
  Buzz ≤ Reputation.** (You decide which hype campaign to let fizzle; warm and full of agency.)
- **Floor:** Reputation never goes below 0; a forced −1 at Rep 0 does nothing. At Rep 0 you hold no
  committed Buzz (cap 0) and simply can't Promote until you Travel (which is never a dead turn and always
  recovers +1) — a self-correcting floor.

**Design verdict — this IS the cleanest rule.** The two alternatives are both worse:
- *"Cap only checked when placing" (over-cap Buzz allowed to persist)* — muddy, and it lets a player lock
  in over-cap Buzz then lose Rep freely = exploitable. Rejected.
- *"An event loss can only reduce unused Rep headroom (never forces Buzz off)"* — perverse: a fully-committed
  player becomes **immune** to Rep-loss events, inverting the tension (commit everything to become
  event-proof). Rejected.
The CD's rule (voluntary gated / forced → return-your-choice) keeps the invariant true at all times with no
perverse incentive, and returning Buzz *by choice* preserves agency. **No better rule found.**

### 1e. Reputation no longer scores (sub-decision 1-ii → Option ii-A)

- The **`3→+2 · 6→+4 · 9→+6 · top→+9` end-VP table is DELETED.** Gating Buzz *replaces* Rep's scoring role;
  letting a Travel-earned resource *also* score would over-reward a travel-spam lane and reintroduce a
  double-dip. The Rep **track stays on the board** as a pure resource track.
- **Scoring is now ONE vector — TICKETS-ONLY** (Change 0). Reputation does not score, **and neither do
  vendors.** The final score IS the **Tour Ticket** win/lose-pile tally. See §Scoring.

### 1f. New tiebreak (knock-on of 1e + Change 0)

> **Most vendors → most regions covered → fewest tickets in your LOSE pile (遺珠).**

(Ranking on Reputation is perverse — it would penalise a player who poured Reach into a winning hype
campaign — so it is dropped. "Most vendors" is a natural first tiebreak under tickets-only: it rewards the
player who built the bigger engine even when two tallies of tickets tie.)

The tickets-only re-sim re-read the five characters (directional read — char vs neutral, so magnitudes are
inflated; read *direction* only). **The field shifted, exactly like the v3→v3.1 deck-re-cost knock-on — a

  four-CD-changes section at top).*** This note and the re-pass's first-region-only A/B are **retired** —

**★ TWO CD-locked changes vs the superseded shape (both sim-validated):**

**(a) Eligibility = `perplayer` (was: first-to-cross-for-the-table).** **Each player earns the boon the
first time THEY cross the mark.** It **cannot pump the leader.** Under the old `first` mode the same player
took *both* marks **~67–80%** of games and was the *only* one who got the fuel — a compounding runaway. The
re-sim showed `perplayer` raises boon-earners from **~1.0 → ~2.0 per mark** (both players get the fuel) →
**zero runaway**, band unchanged (it's small engine-fuel). Simplest to teach; the CD's pick. *(Alternative
`consolation` — first crosser full, later crosser reduced — was near-identical in-sim and keeps a faint
first-mover carrot; held in reserve if the FTUE wants the race to still "mean" something.)*

**(b) The marks are RE-HOMED onto a "Tours Completed" counter** (was: the Bookings/VP score track). With
vendors no longer scoring (Change 0), the old "Bookings 7 / 14" marks sat on a track with no scoring job.
The clean home under tickets-only is the number of **tours you have completed**:

- **The "Tours Completed" counter** = a live count of your **Tour Tickets whose pattern your claimed
  vendors satisfy.** Advance it by 1 the moment a held ticket's pattern is met. **It never goes down** —
  you never lose a won vendor, and every ticket pattern is monotonic in your won vendors, so a completed
  tour stays completed (flip the ticket to its "完成 done" face and slide the counter up). *(This is the
  physical track the old Bookings score lived on, repurposed — one track, relabelled, no new component.)*
- **Marks at your 2nd and 4th completed tour** *(sim knob — `MS_T1=2 / MS_T2=4`; ~1/3 and ~2/3 of a ~6-tour
  game; tune with the character re-sim).*
- **Set A "Tempo & Fuel" boons (kept — reuse existing currencies, no new subsystem):**
  - **Mark 1 (2nd tour) → +1 Reputation (Reach)** — raises your Buzz cap; pure fight-fuel, no points. *(A
    Rep GAIN never triggers the cap edge case.)*
  - **Mark 2 (4th tour) → take 1 ingredient of your choice + your next Bribe costs 1 less ingredient.**

**Why the counter fires DURING play (and why that matters):** because completion is a **live, monotonic,
irreversible** event (patterns are only met by claiming vendors you keep), the counter ticks up the instant
you complete a tour — so the fuel boons land *while the game is open and the fuel is useful*, not lumped at
a phase-judging. This is independent of scoring: a completed tour still waits for its deck's phase to settle
into 成團 for the score (Change 4–5), but it counts toward Rave Reviews *now*. **Edge (FTUE watch, not a
rule to overbuild):** a DAY tour whose pattern you meet only *after* dusk still counts toward the counter
(you completed it) even though it already settled to your LOSE pile at dusk — harmless (the boon is non-VP,
per-player), but note it for the teach.

## Change 3 — Perk field ELIMINATED; vendor table reissued; value = a printed ATTRIBUTE (not a score)

The "perk" is no longer a card element. Once `double-icon`, `wild`, and `+Rep/court` are gone (see the
remap), what's left collapses into **two attributes the card already carries:**

> **A vendor card = a value number + (a region icon | no icon).**

### ★ The vendor-value decision (locked): value is KEPT, as a printed attribute — NOT a live score track

Under tickets-only (Change 0) vendors don't score, so the old live "Bookings" **score track** loses its
job. The CD's directive was to decide whether a vendor still carries a value number at all. **Decision:
KEEP a lightweight vendor *value* number as a printed card attribute.** Reasons:
- **Three sim-validated tickets reference cumulative vendor value** — `d_prestige8` (≥8 value in DAY
  vendors), `n_prestige14` (≥14 total value), `g_prestige12` **揮金如土** (≥12 total value). Dropping value
  would strand these and force a deck redesign the sim did not validate.
- **The deliberate value curve keeps the ★ apex meaningfully special** even without direct scoring: highest
  value = the fastest path to the value-tickets and the marquee "big fish" get (plus the target of the
  ★-tickets `n_star1`). A pure count/pattern deck (no value at all) would flatten the apex into just another
  vendor — worse. So value stays.
- **But it is NOT a live-summed board track anymore.** The value is read off the card. The three
  value-referencing tickets are checked by **summing the printed values of your face-up won vendors at that
  ticket's judging** (vendors sit face-up in your tour — a quick add, and only 3 of ~44 tickets need it).
- **The physical track the old Bookings score lived on is repurposed** as the Rave Reviews "Tours
  Completed" counter (Change 2). One track, relabelled — no new component.

*(Considered and rejected: a fully count/pattern deck with no value number — cleaner on paper, but it
throws away the three validated value-tickets and de-fangs the ★ apex. Value-as-attribute is the smaller,
sim-faithful change.)* **Track/attribute RENAME flagged for bg-theme** — the word "Bookings" no longer
names a score track; it may survive only as the per-card attribute label ("a vendor's Bookings value") or
be renamed (candidates: Draw / Pull / Value). Working label in this spec: **"value."**

The "types" are just the meaningful combinations of value + icon:
- **discount-only** = icon + ordinary value → *engine piece* (daytime food; the ramp).

- **value-only** = no icon + high value → *trophy* (the apex ★; a big value-ticket + marquee get, no ramp).
- **none** = no icon + low value, cost K → *filler* (drinks).

## Scoring — the v6.3 model (TICKETS-ONLY — one vector)

**Your final score is your Tour Ticket tally, and nothing else.** Vendors do not score; Reputation does not
score. A vendor is the ENGINE (its region-discount ramp + the stop you assemble to complete tickets), never
points in itself.

- **Tour Tickets** — **Σ(printed value of each WIN-pile 成團 ticket) − Σ(floor(value ÷ 2) for each LOSE-pile
  遺珠 ticket).** Each claimed ticket is sorted into a pile as its phase closes (DAY at dusk, NIGHT at the
  last train — Change 4/5) and the score is **tallied once at game end** — nothing changes during play.

**Why tickets-only (the CD-locked design change, sim-validated):** under the superseded "both" scoring
(vendor value + tickets) a naive **`ignore_obj`** line — never touch a ticket, just grind the fattest-value
vendors — **won 80.7%** (dominant). Grinding value was pure safe upside; claiming a ticket carried an
opportunity cost *and* a −½ lose-pile downside, so a rational player skipped the whole ticket minigame. That
is a structural flaw, not a knob: the objective layer was strictly a *worse* way to spend the same claims.
Making **tickets the sole score** structurally kills it (a zero-ticket line now scores ~0 and loses 68–69%
h2h; `ignore_obj` falls 80.7% → 43.8%), keeps vendors mechanically central (claims/game unchanged, 9.3),
and de-swings the game (margin 10 → ~7). *(The lighter "just raise ticket values" alternative was tested and
is worse — it scales the reward AND the penalty together, so ignoring tickets stays safe; rejected.)*

**Reputation does NOT score** (Change 1e). **Vendors do NOT score** (Change 0). Highest ticket tally = **Top
Agent** (no sudden death). **Tiebreak: most vendors → most regions covered → fewest LOSE-pile tickets.**
Expected 2p **winner ticket-score ~10–14** *(sim: winner ~12.8 on the expanded deck; margin ~6–7; a sim
knob — much lower absolute numbers than the old value-inclusive ~18–28 band, because value no longer adds
in).*

*(The −½ lose-pile penalty stays a carried table question — the sim can't see the human speculative claim it
disciplines; bg-elegance may cut it if claims feel inert. Unchanged from v6.2. Note: under tickets-only the
penalty matters MORE — it is now the only downside in the whole game, so it directly disciplines "claim it
and hope" over-reaching.)*

## Core loop (v6.3 — a turn = ONE action)

Unchanged from v6.2 except where Change 1 touches it (Travel now gains Rep; Promote is Rep-capped; the
Greedy-Grab option on sourcing). Do **ONE** action, then discard down to Larder cap **7** (recycle rule
unchanged):

- **VISIT THE LOCAL MARKET** *(where you stand)* — flip 3 of this region's ingredient deck, events fire by
  scope, **keep up to 2** (媽寶/大胃王 per kit). **Greedy Grab option:** keep +1 more → **−1 Reputation**
  (only if it wouldn't break your Buzz cap).
- **CONVENIENCE STORE** *(anywhere)* — flip the top 1 of any region's deck; ingredient → take it, event →
  fires by scope. **Greedy Grab option:** take the next card too → **−1 Reputation** (same cap check).
- **TRAVEL** — move to a neighbour (HSR = N↔S), flip the arriving region's top card (event whiffs), **and
  gain +1 Reputation** (raises your Buzz cap — travel is now the engine's fuel).
- **PROMOTE** *(with the vendor, in this region)* — place **2 Buzz** (網紅: 3) from supply onto co-located
  vendor(s), **provided your total committed Buzz stays ≤ your Reputation.**
- **BRIBE A VENDOR** — out-hype your rival on the spot (≥2 majority; 大哥 ≥1) + pay ingredients (− region
  discounts) → win it: it joins your tour as an **engine piece** (its region-icon feeds the discount ramp;
  it may **complete a held ticket** → advance your Tours-Completed counter), its Buzz clears to supply
  (freeing your cap), refill the slot, move the vendor-claims clock +1, lock the rival out. **No points, no
  Rep grant, no perk** — a vendor is just a value attribute + (icon | no icon), and **vendors do NOT score**
  (§Scoring).
- **CLAIM A TOUR TICKET** — take one from the face-up row of 3 (the current phase's DAY or NIGHT pool); it
  refills. Judged into your 成團 / 遺珠 piles at the end of its deck's phase — **the sole scoring vector.**
