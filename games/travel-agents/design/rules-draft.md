## 0. The pitch (read aloud, 20 seconds)
"You're rival **travel agents** building the best Taiwan food **tour** in one eating day. Ride between four
regions to spread your name, dig ingredients from each market, and pile **Buzz** on the vendors you want —
but **your hype can't outrun your reputation.** Win a vendor by **out-hyping your rival on the spot** — each
one makes the next cheaper and becomes a stop on your tours. Your points come from **Tour Tickets** —
line them up and finish them before **dusk** flips the street to the neon **night market**, and again
before the last train's called. Most tour points is **Top Agent**."

## 1. Goal
Score the most points — **and points come from one place only: your Tour Tickets** (your win-pile 成團 minus
half your lose-pile 遺珠 — §9). Most = **Top Agent**. **Vendors don't score** — a vendor is your *engine*
(each makes its region's ingredients cheaper) and the *stop* you assemble to complete tickets. *(**Reputation**
doesn't score either — it's the fuel that lets you make Buzz; see §4.)*

## 2. Setup (teach while you set up)
1. Unfold the **Taiwan map** — four regions in a ring, **North 北 · Central 中 · South 南 · East 東**, an HSR
   spine linking North↔South. It holds the **day-clock track** (a **dusk** mark and a **last-train** mark),
   the **Tours Completed** counter (a small track with two **Rave Review** milestone marks — at your **2nd**
   and **4th** completed tour), the **Reputation** track (your Buzz-cap fuel — *not* a score), and — **per
   region** — a face-down **vendor deck** with **face-up vendor slots** (**North 2, the others 1**) and a
   face-down **ingredient market deck**. Set each **Tours Completed** marker to 0 and each **Reputation**
   marker to **2**. *(No score track — your score is your Tour Tickets, tallied at the end; §9.)*
2. Sort each region's cards into a **DAY deck** and a **NIGHT deck** (drinks are the low-value vendors mixed
   into each); set the **night** decks aside for dusk. Shuffle each **day vendor deck** and **day ingredient
   deck** face-down into place. Ingredient decks use the 5-palette (**N 豬肉 · C 茶 · S 海鮮 · E 米** + the
   common **K 糖**, in **C & S** only) plus a few **event cards** (§6). Fill the face-up vendor slots. Set out
   the **Buzz** supply (~24 @2p).
3. **Tour Tickets — two decks, DAY and NIGHT:** shuffle the **DAY** deck, **draw 5** (rest to the box),
   reveal **3** as the shared **ticket row** (the other 2 form its draw pile). Set the **NIGHT** deck aside
   for dusk (drawn the same way then). A ticket's **deck is its clock**: **DAY tickets are judged at dusk,
   NIGHT tickets at the last train** (§8–§9).
4. Each agent takes an **Agent Character** (ability + flaw — read aloud, public) and a **pawn** (start
   opposite at 2p — one North, one South). Your Larder starts empty and stays **face-up** all game.

## 3. How a turn works (the core loop — teach these six, then just play)
Do **ONE** action. Then if your Larder holds over **7** ingredients, discard down to 7 (to that region's
market discard, which reshuffles in when the deck runs dry — ingredients are never removed from the game).

- **VISIT THE LOCAL MARKET** *(where you stand)* — flip the top **3** of this region's ingredient deck. Any
  **event** happens now (by its scope — §6), then is discarded. **Keep up to 2** ingredients, discard the
  rest. **Greedy Grab (optional):** keep **1 more** (3) → **−1 Reputation** (§4a).
- **CONVENIENCE STORE** *(anywhere)* — flip the top **1** card of **any** region's ingredient deck: an
  ingredient → take it; an event → it fires **by its scope** (§6). **Greedy Grab (optional):** take the
  **next card too** (2 blind) → **−1 Reputation** (§4a). *(Any region, no travel — the reliable way to grab
  the common **K**.)*
- **TRAVEL** — move to a **neighbouring** region (HSR = North↔South), then flip the top **1** card of the
  region you **arrive** in: ingredient → take it; **event → nothing, keep moving**. **You gain +1
  Reputation** — getting around spreads your name (§4).
- **PROMOTE** *(with the vendor, in this region)* — place **2 Buzz** from the supply onto vendor(s) you're
  with here, **so long as your total committed Buzz stays ≤ your Reputation** (§4). Buzz stays on the card
  for all to see; your rival can pile on too.
- **BRIBE A VENDOR** — win a face-up vendor by out-hyping your rival, on the spot (§4–§5). *(Winning any
  vendor moves the day-clock +1 and clears your Buzz off it — freeing your Reputation cap again.)*
- **LINE UP A TOUR TICKET** — take one ticket from the face-up **row of 3** (the current phase's DAY or
  NIGHT pool) as an open commitment; refill the row. You may only take one you **haven't yet completed**. It
  scores later into your win-pile / lose-pile (§8–§9) — **your tickets are your whole score.** *(The agent
  lines up tours; the customers book them — completing a tour is the win.)*

> **Agent Characters bend the rules.** Each mat carries **one ability + one flaw** for that agent alone
> (e.g. the competitive eater keeps all 3 on a Visit) — read every mat aloud at setup; a mat wins where it
> differs from the above.

## 4. Buzz & Reputation — the hype fight (the heart of it)
You show a vendor you want them with **Buzz** — you promise crowds and hype (your job: bring people *to* the
food).
- Buzz only ever sits **in the supply** or **on a vendor** — never in your hand.
- To Buzz a vendor you must be **standing in its region**.
- **Both agents can pile Buzz on the same vendor** — that's the fight, and it's where the game is won.

> **Your hype can't outrun your reputation.** Your **total Buzz committed on the board may never exceed your
> current Reputation.** Reputation is your standing (a marker on the Reputation track, starting at **2**) —
> **not a score**, just how much Buzz you're allowed out at once. **Travel +1** (getting around spreads your
> name) and a few **events** raise it; **winning a vendor clears your Buzz off it**, freeing your cap for the
> next campaign. So to out-escalate a contested vendor you first need the Reputation to cover the stack —
> *travel builds the ammunition.*

### 4a. Reputation sinks — greed costs you (the Greedy Grab)
When you source, you may **grab one extra ingredient beyond your limit** (an extra keep on a **Visit**, or a
second card on a **Convenience Store**) — each grab costs **−1 Reputation** (you shoved to the front of the
queue; word gets around). A couple of **events** also cost Reputation (a bad review, a mix-up).

> **The one edge-case rule (say it once):** your committed Buzz can never exceed your Reputation. So a
> **Greedy Grab you can't afford is simply not allowed** (you may only take it if it wouldn't drop your
> Reputation below your committed Buzz); and if an **event** drops your Reputation below your committed Buzz,
> **immediately return Buzz from the board to the supply — your choice which — until you're back within your
> cap.** (Reputation never goes below 0.)

## 5. Bribing a vendor (winning it over) — ONE rule for every vendor
To **Bribe** a face-up vendor, all at once:
1. be **with the vendor, in its region**;
2. hold **at least 2 more Buzz on it than your rival** (the majority, checked right now);
3. **pay its ingredients**, minus your region discounts (below).
Take the vendor to your tour — it's now a **stop on your tours** and an **engine piece**. If it completes a
Tour Ticket you hold (its pattern is now met), flip that ticket to its **done** side and **advance your
Tours Completed counter** (§5, Rave Reviews). Then **clear ALL Buzz on it** (yours *and* your rival's) back
to the supply — **freeing your Reputation cap** — **refill** the slot from that region's current deck, and
**move the day-clock +1** (§7). The vendor is locked from your rival. **Winning a vendor scores no points on
its own** — points come only from completed Tour Tickets (§9).

> **A vendor card is just two things:** a **value** number and **either a region icon or none.** No perks,
> no bonus tracks — **and the value is not a score.** Its value only matters for the few Tour Tickets that
> ask for it (e.g. *"vendors totalling ≥12 value"*) and marks out the big-ticket prizes. **Each region icon
> you hold** makes that region's ingredients **1 cheaper** on future vendors — sign a couple of Southern
> stalls and the next barely needs oysters (that's the engine). **Drinks and the apex ★ trophies carry no
> icon** (drinks = cheap low-value filler; ★ = the top-value marquee get, no ramp). **Buzz is never
> discounted**, so the *hype fight* — gated by your Reputation — not the ingredients, is what gates your
> next big win.

> **Outraced, not robbed.** If your rival out-hypes you, your Buzz just fizzles back to the supply — you keep
> every ingredient you sourced, and a fresh vendor comes out. You never seize a place.

> **Rave Reviews (milestones).** As you complete tours, your **Tours Completed** counter climbs. **The first
> time YOU reach your 2nd completed tour → +1 Reputation; the first time YOU reach your 4th → take 1
> ingredient of your choice + your next Bribe costs 1 less ingredient.** Each is a one-time boon, **and each
> agent earns their own** (crossing a mark never pumps the leader — you can only ever earn your own). Your
> tour's getting noticed — a little tailwind, never points.

## 6. The market decks & events (the island doesn't sit still)
Each region's market is a **face-down deck** of its **signature** ingredient (+ the common **K**, in two of
the four regions) and a few **event** cards (mostly hazards). **Working a market — a Visit or a Convenience
Store — fires any flipped event straight away, by its scope; a card flipped as you *arrive* on a Travel does
nothing.**
- **Hazards** *(the majority)* — a typhoon, a "完售 SOLD OUT" sign, scooter gridlock. They **tax or delay —
  never destroy** what you hold, never cost a whole turn.
- **Boons** *(the minority)* — a festival that hands out Buzz, a kind auntie, or a vendor **suddenly gets
  hot** (★ below).

> **The scope tag — who the event hits**, measured from the region the card came out of: **⦿ You** (just the
> flipper) · **⬢ This region** (everyone standing in the drawn region) · **➤ Next door** (someone in a
> neighbour). *Worked example:* on a **Visit** you stand in the region you flipped, so ⬢ hits **you**; on a
> **Convenience Store** you're often elsewhere, so ⬢ hits **whoever stands in the region you flipped** — flip
> a rival's region and a scoped hazard can land on **them**. Never a personal attack — just the island living
> its life.

> **★ Star vendors.** Some night-market names carry a **printed ★** (a real Michelin / Bib-Gourmand nod),
> carry the top **value**, carry **no region icon** (a pure trophy, not an engine piece), and sit one apex
> per region — the rotating prize both agents chase (they finish the value-tickets fastest and are the
> marquee "get"). A boon can also drop a **★ Star token** on a vendor, making it worth **+2 value** to
> whoever wins it.

## 7. The day-clock — Day → Night (every vendor you win moves it)
It's **one eating day**. The **day-clock** climbs **+1 every time anyone Bribes a vendor** — food or drink
alike. It has two marks (2p: **dusk = the 5th table-wide vendor win · last train = the 10th**; the marks
grow with more players):
- **Act I — Daytime 白天:** morning food + the day's drinks. Cheap and low-contest at first — build your
  discount ramp and stake early positions. **Daytime Tour Tickets** are on offer.
- **DUSK (the day-clock hits the dusk mark) — the street turns over.** In one flip: **judge every claimed
  DAYTIME ticket** (completed → win-pile · unfinished → lose-pile — §9); then **clear the entire day layer
  island-wide** — every face-up **and** face-down **day vendor deck AND day ingredient deck** — and replace
  it with each region's **night decks** (a neon recolour, run **event-heavier**). Swap the ticket row to the
  **nighttime** pool. **You keep your Larder.** Any unclaimed day vendor is gone.
- **Act II — Night Market 夜市:** the big **night-market stalls** — expensive, fought-over, the ★ apex per
  region. This is where the tour is won.

*(The faster you all win vendors, the faster the day moves — a built-in catch-up for a slow, tall player.)*

## 8. Ending the trip — the last train home
When the day-clock hits the **last-train mark** (2p: the **10th vendor win**), everyone catches the last
train: **finish the current round, play one more full round, and stop.** *(Backstop: if a region's deck
empties, its slot isn't refilled until the next flip; if all decks dry island-wide, the phase ends then. If
one win trips **dusk and** the last train at once, resolve **dusk first**, then the end check.)*

## 9. Scoring — reveal your piles and tally once
**Your score is your Tour Tickets, and nothing else.** Reveal your two piles and tally once, at game end:
- **+ the printed value of each ticket in your WIN pile (成團)** — the tours that ran.
- **− floor(value ÷ 2) for each ticket in your LOSE pile (遺珠)** — half its own value, rounded down — the
  ones that got away.

Tickets are sorted into your own two piles as each phase ends (DAY at dusk, NIGHT at the last train —
§7–§8); nothing changes during play, and you tally **once**.

**Vendors do NOT score** — every vendor you won was your engine (cheaper ingredients) and a stop you used to
complete tickets; its value was never points. **Reputation does NOT score** — it was your Buzz-cap fuel all
game, nothing more.

Most points wins → **Top Agent**. **Ties: most vendors, then most regions covered, then fewest tickets in
your LOSE pile (遺珠).**

## 10. The warm-tone reminders (margin asides, not rules)
- You're **promoting** Taiwan's food — winning a vendor is out-hyping your rival to earn their blessing,
  never taking from them. "Bribe" is the cheeky word; the deed is a warm hard sell, not corruption.
- Events are the island living its life — never sacred customs for laughs; a Convenience-Store event on your
  rival is the *island* doing it, a shared groan, never a spiteful strike. Dish and vendor names stay real
  (Tâi-lô + 繁體); the comedy is the caper gag and your own gluttony.
