## Identity status: COHERENT, and this tightens it

All five asks pull the *same* direction — a legible, fully-diegetic **travel-agency marketing funnel** that
the current game only half-named. Restraint rung is unchanged (**evocative theme**); no lore added, no new
component type, no new sensitive motif. The through-line these changes make explicit:

> **Travel** (spread your name) → **Reach** (your standing/credibility) → **Buzz** (the queue you drum up at a
> stall) → win the **Vendor** onto your tour → **Bookings** (customers your tour pulls in = score), with
> **Rave Reviews** as the "your tour's blowing up" milestones and **Tour Tickets** the specific tours you
> commit to running (成團 they ran / 遺珠 they got away).

Every rung is a distinct noun with a distinct game job. That is the opposite of muddle — it's a funnel a
new player could half-recite. Verdict: these changes make the theme *more* integrated, not less.

## 1. Prestige → **Bookings** — CONFIRM (a diegetic upgrade)

**Validation against the identity.** "Prestige" is a generic Euro-abstraction word that never belonged to
the travel-agency fiction — it was the one scoring vector with no in-world referent. **"Bookings" is the
travel industry's own success metric** (a tour agency lives and dies by its bookings), and it reads
perfectly against the north star ("build the island's best food tour → most people book it → Top Agent").
The mechanic survives untouched: a better vendor (the beef-noodle king, the ★ apex stall) is a bigger draw,
so it pulls **more bookings** — "the oyster omelette everyone's queuing for → more people book the tour to
get it." Clean, diegetic, and it makes the win condition legible: **most Bookings = you built the tour people
most wanted = you win the season.**

**It also earns coherence elsewhere.** A tour "runs" (成團, the win pile) precisely *when enough people book
it* — so the score (Bookings) and the win pile (成團) now share one travel-agency logic. That's free
resonance the word "Prestige" never gave us.

**One light sensitivity note (not a flag).** "Customers/tourists as your score" must stay on the
anti-extraction rail: keep the framing **"people you bring *to* the island's food"** (sharing, celebration),
never "packaging the island for outsiders." The rail already handles this (local promoters showing off home);
Bookings inherits it cleanly. No new concern.

**Verdict: CONFIRM "Bookings."** Strictly better than Prestige.

## 2. Bookings ↔ Tour Tickets adjacency — **THEY COEXIST; discipline the verb, don't rename**

The CD's working read is right, and I'd sharpen it into a rule. The two are **conceptually distinct**:

- **Tour Ticket** = a *specific tour you commit to running* — the plan/itinerary (a card; the objective).
  Pays out via 成團 / 遺珠.
- **Bookings** = *the customers your whole agency pulled in* — the headline score (a track marker).

**The real collision is not the nouns — it's the verb "book."** "Book a Tour Ticket" (claim an objective)
would clash with "Bookings" (the score). The fix is a clean, *diegetically perfect* verb split that the
travel trade hands us for free:

> **The AGENT lines up tours; the CUSTOMERS book them.** Reserve "book / booked / Bookings" for the
> customer/score side ONLY. For claiming a Tour Ticket, the flavour verb becomes **"line up a tour"** /
> **"lock in a tour"** / **"take on a tour"** (mechanical verb stays "claim"). Retire "book a tour" as the
> claim verb.

With that one discipline, the nouns are safe — and **component distinctness does the rest**: Tour Tickets are
cards in a face-up row that sort into vivid 成團 / 遺珠 piles; Bookings is a track marker. Physically nothing
alike, so "my tickets" never blurs into "my bookings" at the table.

This is also a genuine **ally, not a rival**: your Tour Tickets are *the tours you're offering this season*;
your Bookings are *the customers those tours pulled in*. Win great vendors → your tours are irresistible →
customers book → Bookings ↑.

**Firm recommendation: KEEP both names.** Do **not** rename Tour Tickets — it's locked, load-bearing (the
sim vocabulary, the decision-log vocabulary, the named Ticket-to-Ride ancestor), and the collision is fully
solved by the verb split + component distinctness. **If** the CD wants zero "book"-adjacency at any cost, the
*cheap* rename is the brand-new VP track, not the wired-in Ticket (fallback labels: **Guests / Travellers**) —
but I recommend against it: "Bookings" is the better, more diegetic word and the verb rule is enough.

## 3. The two per-player piles — LOCK **成團 "Tours That Ran"** (win) / **遺珠 "The Ones That Got Away"** (lose)

They now live per-player on the character mat: a WIN pile (completed tours) and a LOSE pile (unfinished).

**LOSE pile — CONFIRM as-is: 遺珠 · "The Ones That Got Away."** Warm, rueful, affectionate; the travel
agent's wistful glance at the trip that almost was, never a failure-scold. Perfect. (Native-review nuance to
carry: 遺珠 = "the overlooked pearl" — idiomatically warm via 遺珠之憾; the exact travel-trade antonym of 成團
is the clunkier, colder **不成團** — we deliberately chose 遺珠 for *tone* over precision. Native reviewer to
confirm 遺珠 reads natural here, or offer a softer travel phrase.)

**WIN pile — IMPROVE the English skin: "Published Tours" → "Tours That Ran."** 成團 (chéng-tuán) is the real
travel-agency word for a tour that **filled and departed** (vs 不成團, cancelled for want of sign-ups). "Published
Tours" mistranslates it toward *brochure/catalogue*; the accurate, warmer, more vivid reading is that the
tour **ran** — it happened, it departed. "Tours That Ran" also pairs cleanly with the lose pile:

> **成團 "Tours That Ran"** ↔ **遺珠 "The Ones That Got Away"** — *ran* vs *got away*: a crisp, warm antonym
> pair a table reads instantly.

(For a one-word infodesign label where space is tight: **"Departures."** Keep "Tours That Ran" as the
player-facing name.) This also closes the loop with **Bookings**: a tour *runs* when enough people *book* it.

**Sensitivity/native:** 成團 / chéng-tuán and 遺珠 / yí-zhū both go to the native reviewer to bless (already
flagged); 繁體 rides alongside the English as the flavour anchor.

**Verdict:** LOSE pile confirmed; WIN pile English skin improved to "Tours That Ran" (繁體 成團 unchanged).

## 4. Coherence check on the two new mechanics (bg-design speccing in parallel)

### 4a. Reputation gates Buzz — FICTION CONFIRMED (strong); LABEL: rename **Reputation → Reach**

**The fiction hangs together — this is some of the strongest theme↔mechanic resonance in the game.** The CD's
read is correct and I'd only sharpen it. The chain:

> **Travel** = getting your agency's name around the island (legwork, being seen) → builds your standing.
> **Events** = getting featured / picked up by the island's happenings → also builds your standing.
> Your standing then **gates how much hype (Buzz) you can generate** — you can't summon a busload to a stall
> if nobody's heard of you; a trusted, far-carrying agency's word moves crowds.

Needing standing *before* you can create hype is genuinely inevitable fiction (it's the real influencer/
travel-media funnel: audience/credibility gates engagement), and **"travel spreads the word"** does double
duty — it finally makes travel *thematically* load-bearing (travel = building your name, not just fetching
ingredients), which helps the design's chronic "pull travel" problem from the fiction side.

**Label: rename "Reputation" → "Reach."** Now that the track has a *job*, "Reputation" (accurate but dry, and
it doesn't signal how it differs from Buzz — both just sound like "fame") is no longer the best word. **Reach**
is:

- **The earn is inevitable with it:** you *travel → extend your Reach* across the island. Travel literally
  *is* reaching more places. No other candidate ties to the earn-mechanic this tightly.
- **It sits cleanly one rung above Buzz in a real funnel:** **Reach → Buzz → Bookings** is the actual
  marketing pipeline (audience → engagement → conversions). Three distinct nouns, three distinct jobs; the
  word-order teaches the mechanic.
- **On-register:** the game is explicitly modern (網紅, "going viral," neon/scooters/HSR), so modern-marketing
  vocabulary fits and reads warm/funny in the influencer-caper key — not a dry Euro track.
- **Scoring still works:** if Reach also scores by end position, "biggest Reach = best-known agency" reads
  fine and ties to Bookings (big Reach → more Bookings).

Runner-up: **Clout** (punchy, "you've got the clout to move crowds"; but travel→Clout is a looser link than
travel→Reach). Conservative/low-churn option: **keep "Reputation"** (safe, but the dry word and the weak
travel-tie are exactly what the re-job exposes).

**One coherence question for bg-design (not my call, but flag it):** is Reach now a **gate only**, a **scored
track only**, or **both**? The fiction supports all three (standing opens doors *now* AND is part of your
season legacy), but the player-aid must state its one job plainly — a track that both gates a currency and
scores can muddle if unlabelled. "Reach" carries any of the three cleanly; just pick one and say it.

### 4b. Bookings-track milestone boons — NAME: **"Rave Reviews"** (好評)

First agency past each mark on the Bookings track earns a boon. Fiction: as bookings climb, your tour hits
press milestones — it gets reviewed, it trends, it blows up. Warm, celebratory "your tour's taking off" beats,
fully coherent with the five-star-review flavour already baked into the Bribe reframe ("a glowing five-star
review") and the ★-accolade world.

**Name: "Rave Reviews."** Each mark is a review threshold; the first agency past it earns the **Rave** (the
boon). Travel-native (tours live on reviews), warm, and it slots beside the existing review/★ vocabulary.

**Two collisions I deliberately avoided** (both were on the CD's example radar):
- **"Going Viral"** is taken — it's 網紅's ability name. Don't reuse it for the milestones.
- **"Sold Out" / 完售** is taken — it's an event *hazard* flavour (a 完售 sign landing on a rival). Don't reuse
  the sold-out beat as a *positive* milestone; it reads inverted.

繁體 anchor: **好評** (hǎo-píng, "a rave / glowing review") — distinct from 爆紅 (already used for the ★-gets-hot
boon). Native-review it. Alt umbrella name if the CD dislikes "Rave Reviews": **"Headlines"** (your tour makes
headlines). Individual marks *may* carry escalating flavour (first review → featured → trending) as an
infodesign polish, but restraint says lock ONE umbrella name; don't grow the glossary.

Nothing in this pass touches the load-bearing rails, and it adds no new sensitive motif:
- **Bribe / 大哥 warmth** — untouched; Bookings/Reach/Rave Reviews are all celebratory and keep the smarm on
  the agent, never the vendor/place.

- **繁體 / Tâi-lô discipline** — the only new/changed 繁體 are **成團 · 遺珠 · 好評**; all three go to the native
  reviewer with the existing list (成團/遺珠 already flagged; add 好評).
- **Anti-extraction glance on "Bookings"** — keep the "bringing people *to* the food = sharing/celebration"
  framing; do not let "customers as score" tip toward "packaging Taiwan for outsiders." Rail already covers it.

## Anti-drift / anti-restriction note

This is the healthy case: mechanics moved (Reputation got a new job; a milestone layer + a renamed score
appeared) and the fiction **followed and tightened** rather than fighting back. No re-theme was needed to
protect a stale story — the funnel language absorbed every change. The one thing to *resist* is glossary
creep: hold ONE name per rung (Reach, Buzz, Bookings, Rave Reviews, Tour Ticket, 成團/遺珠) and kill any
synonym that drifts in.
