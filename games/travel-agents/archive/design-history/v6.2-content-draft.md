## The v6.2 model (recap — what this content is built for)

- **Region vendor markets.** Each of the 4 regions (**North 北 · Central 中 · South 南 · East 東**) has, per
  phase, its own face-down **vendor deck** — a **DAY vendor deck** in Act I, a separate **NIGHT vendor deck**
  in Act II — with **drinks mixed in as the low-value vendors** in each (no special status — just cheap
  vendors).
- **Face-up display:** **North shows 2, the others show 1** (tunable), refilled in place from that region's
  current deck. So 5 vendors are courtable across the board at any moment.
- **THE DUSK FLIP — everything turns over to night (v6.2, CD 2026-08-07).** At **dusk**, the *entire* day
  layer is cleared island-wide — **both the day VENDOR decks AND the day INGREDIENT market decks are removed
  (not just the face-up cards)** — and replaced by the **night decks**, the ticket pool switches to night,
  and the card **colour/mood swaps** (warm morning → neon night market). One big, teachable, thematic
  "the street turns over" moment; any unclaimed day vendor is gone (real Act-I urgency). **Players KEEP their
  Larder** across the flip — only the board/supply resets, so Act I sourcing still pays into the night.
- **The night half is HOTTER (the "bustling night market").** The **night ingredient decks use the SAME 5
  palette but are EVENT-HEAVIER** (more scoped events firing = crowds/chaos/interaction) — the hectic feel
  lives in event density + the recolour, **not** in new ingredient types (so the kept Larder still spends).
  Night event density (and whether the night half is a shorter sprint) → sim-tunable.
- **One courting rule** for everything: on-site (in the region) + a **≥2 Buzz majority** + **pay ingredients**
  (food carries region-discount icons; drinks don't). *(大哥 needs only a **≥1** margin — see the kit table.)*
- **The VENDOR-CLAIMS clock (LOCKED — the mission-clock A/B is closed).** The day advances **each time any
  player claims a vendor**: **dusk = the 5th table-wide vendor claim, the last train = the 10th @2p**
  (tunable; the marks grow with player count — see Player-count scaling). Deck-exhaustion backstops end a
  phase if the decks dry island-wide. *(Chosen over the mission clock: tighter and more reliable — sim sd
  3.9 vs 4.9, crisp mid-game dusk at ~turn 21 vs ~34, ~14% vs 26% stall.)*
- **Tour Tickets = a face-up row of 3**, refilled when claimed (claiming = a full action); drawn from that
  phase's set. **Scored via the WIN-PILE / LOSE-PILE model** (see §Scoring below) — no live retroactive
  adjustment.
- **Variable setup (replayability):** **12 daytime + 12 nighttime** missions live in the box; **draw 5 per
  phase per game**, the rest return to the box. (+ a small **general** pool that seeds both — see below.)
- **Ingredients — the 5-token palette:** **N 豬肉 pork · C 茶 tea · S 海鮮 seafood · E 米 rice · K 糖 common**
  (K in **C & S** only). Sourced via Visit / Convenience Store (fires scoped events) / Travel — from a **day
  ingredient deck** (Act I) then a separate **event-heavier night ingredient deck** (Act II) per region.
- **Larder cap 7; discards RECYCLE (the anti-lockout rule).** Over 7 → discard the excess to **that region's
  market discard**, which **reshuffles back into the deck when it runs dry** — ingredients are **renewable,
  never removed from the game**, so a hoarder **can't permanently lock anyone out** (hoarding is also
  self-taxing: past 7 you just dig-then-discard = wasted actions). Day discards clear with the day layer at
  dusk. *Temporary* denial (sit on a region's scarce signature to slow a rival) is a real tactic → the knob
  is the **per-region signature supply size**; the sim's hoarder probe confirms it's a trap, not a lockout.

## Scoring — the WIN-PILE / LOSE-PILE model (LOCKED, CD directive)

Final score at the last train = **Tour Prestige** (sum of your claimed vendors' values) + **Reputation**
end-VP (by final track position: 3→+2 · 6→+4 · 9→+6 · top→+9) + your **Tour-Ticket tally**.

**Tour Tickets do NOT score live.** A claimed ticket sits in front of you as an open commitment. Its outcome
is **locked at the end of its phase**, then the card is moved to one of two piles:

- **Daytime tickets are judged at DUSK; nighttime tickets at the LAST TRAIN** (a general-pool ticket is
  judged at the end of whichever phase's row it was claimed from). At that moment: **completed → your WIN
  pile · unfinished → your LOSE pile.** *(This gives daytime tickets real Act-I urgency — coherent with the
  dusk flip that sweeps away the day vendors: finish it before the street turns over, or it's lost.)*
- **Points are tallied ONCE, at game end** (nothing is added or subtracted during play):
  > **Ticket score = Σ(printed value of each WIN-pile ticket) − Σ( floor(value ÷ 2) for each LOSE-pile ticket ).**
- **The −½ penalty reading is LOCKED as "half the ticket's own printed value, rounded down" (floor(value/2))**
  — *not* a flat half-point each. This is exactly what the v6.2 sim modelled, which is why the whole change
  is **accounting-only and sim-equivalent**: the same final numbers, arrived at by two piles + one tally
  instead of running mid-game adjustments.
- **You may only claim a ticket you have not yet completed** (prevents a free-points-for-an-action edge
  case; FAQ item from the sim).

**Why this model (CD rationale):** far simpler accounting (no retroactive ± bookkeeping every time a ticket
finishes or fails — just sort into two piles and total once) and it *shortens* the scoring prose, helping
the standing rulebook-over-cap flag. **It is NOT a balance change** — the same scores result, so no re-sim.
**Naming (flag for bg-theme / bg-infodesign, not locked here):** the two piles want a warm theme skin — e.g.
**"published tours"** (win) vs **"the ones that got away"** (lose).

*(The penalty itself stays a carried table question: the sim saw no behavioural difference with/without it
because bots only claim finishable tickets — its real job is disciplining a human's **speculative** claim,
which only the Screentop session can judge. Kept for now; bg-elegance may cut if claims feel inert.)*

## Balance framework — how the board is tuned to PULL travel

**Principle: value alone kills travel (everyone camps the rich region). Travel must be a distributed
*pull*.** Four levers, applied below:

1. **One apex (★) vendor per region** in the nighttime layer, of *comparable* value — so the single best
   prize on the board **rotates by region**, dragging both agents around at night.
2. **A cross-region recipe cycle** on the apex/premium vendors: **N needs S · S needs E · E needs N**, with
   **C** as the hub (needs S or N). You must *travel to source* the second ingredient. This deliberately
   routes traffic **through East** (the region prior sims kept starving).
3. **North is dense, not rich.** North shows **2** face-up (the busy capital) but its apex isn't the
   highest; the scarcer premium draws sit in the 1-slot regions, so North's extra visibility is balanced by
   the others holding rarer prizes.
4. **Drinks are cheap where the common lives.** Drinks cost **K (sugar)**; **C & S carry K locally** (cheap,
   convenient drinks — the bubble-tea belt), while **N & E** drinks need K sourced via CS/Travel (a bit
   dearer). So each region reads differently: *come to C/S for an easy drink, come to N/E for the food.*

## Player-count scaling (v6.2 — bg-architect draft; **2p is the design home**)

**2p is the design home and the focus of the first v6.2 sim.** 3–4p is a scaling delta to finalize with
`bg-architect` *after* the 2p base locks — but the sim is parametrised by count, so a **light per-count
seat-balance smoke** rides along cheaply. Scaling principles:

- **The vendor-claims clock scales with players (the CD's lever, now on vendor claims).** The **claims-to-
  dusk** and **claims-to-the-last-train** marks GROW with count (roughly per-player — ~5 claims/player to the
  last train, dusk at the midpoint) so a 3–4p game doesn't end in a flash. And the **vendor + ticket SUPPLY
  scales with it** (more face-up slots / a bigger phase deck; more tickets drawn per phase), so there are
  enough vendors to court and tours to book. *Rough proposal (tune — bg-architect finalizes with the MP
  pass):* dusk / last-train claims **~5 / 10 (2p) → ~7–8 / 15 (3p) → ~10 / 20 (4p)**; ticket draw **5/phase
  (2p) → 6–7 (3–4p)**.
- **Board scales:** face-up vendor slots (≈ +1 total per extra player), **Buzz supply** (≈ +8–10/player),
  per-region deck sizes (a little larger), start pawns spread around the ring.
- **The dedicated multiplayer pass (DEFERRED until the 2p base locks)** tests what the smoke can't:
  **seat/turn-order balance**, **KINGMAKING** (at 3–4p the *disruptive* / *hoarder* / scoped-CS levers can
  decide who wins **between the other players** — the #1 MP watch), and **downtime** (keep turns snappy —
