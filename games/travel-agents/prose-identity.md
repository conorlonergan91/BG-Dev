# ✍️ Travel Agents: Taiwan — Prose Identity (the VOICE BIBLE)

> The living source of truth for the game's **voice** — the way every player-facing word sounds.
> Third canonical reference alongside **`identity.md`** (the felt/game identity) and
> **`art/art-style.md`** (the visual identity). If `art-style.md` says how the game *looks*, this says
> how it *reads*. Owned by `bg-copywriter`; written to serve `identity.md`'s tone; never changes a
> rule's meaning (that's `bg-design` / `bg-elegance`).
>
> **Citation convention (read this first, use it every time).** Every copy or writing brief in this
> project — flavour, card copy, backstories, box lines, rulebook asides — **opens by citing this
> file**, exactly the way every art brief opens by citing `art-style.md`. That one habit is what keeps
> the whole project sounding like one narrator. If you're about to write a player-facing word and you
> haven't reread the core voice below, stop and reread it.

---

## 1. The core voice — one statement

**An over-enthusiastic travel buddy narrating a "top-secret" snack mission — warm, deadpan, and always
the punchline himself.**

Six adjectives: **warm · affectionate · deadpan · specific · absurd · celebratory.**

**The single most important rule — the one that governs everything else:**

> **The joke always lands on the over-the-top travel-agent character. Never on the locals, the vendors,
> the food, or the culture — those stay dignified, real, and beloved.**

We laugh *with* Taiwan, never *at* it. The comedy is the grown adult in a trench coat over a Hawaiian
shirt taking a bowl of noodles as seriously as a heist. The auntie pressing a third ladle on you, the
oyster omelette, the 完售 sold-out sign, the last bus — those are true, warm, and told straight. The
gag is the *agent's* reaction to them.

**The one test** (from `identity.md`, load-bearing): *would a Taiwanese player laugh **with** this, or
feel it laughs **at** their home?* If a line only works because you find Taiwan itself strange or
foreign, cut it. **Secular always** — no temple/deity/ghost-month/funerary motifs as decoration or
gags.

## 2. Tone by surface

Each writing surface holds the *same* narrator but at a slightly different volume. One-liner + a
gold-standard example for each.

### Character mat quote — *the punchy deadpan one-liner*
A single line in the character's own voice. Deadpan, self-assured, the humour in the gap between how
seriously they take themselves and how ordinary the thing is. **One line. No setup, no explanation.**

> "You call it over-packing. Mum calls it being prepared." *(The Homebody)*
> "I've personally taste-tested the entire island. More than once." *(The Big Eater)*
> "Keep up if you can, dear — I already booked tomorrow." *(The Globetrotter)*

### Character backstory — *escalating comic rhythm* (the gold standard)
Short lines. **Rule-of-three escalation**, then a **deadpan turn**, then **end on a wink**. The
character is affectionately ridiculous; the world around them is warm and real. This is the register
the CD called "much better" — mine it.

> Breakfast becomes second breakfast.
> Second breakfast becomes an "important cultural tasting."
> Lunch somehow lasts until dinner.
>
> By sunset everyone's carrying food babies and questioning their life choices while she's cheerfully
> asking, "Dessert?"
>
> She's personally inspected every famous stall in Taiwan. Several times. For quality assurance,
> obviously. *(The Big Eater)*

Note the shape: three escalating beats → a deadpan turn ("food babies") → the wink that also **hints
why she's good to play** ("inspected every famous stall … for quality assurance"). That last move is
§4's *wink at the strategy*.

### Card flavour (events / bookings / vendors) — *wry, specific, short*
One grounded Taiwan detail, told with a light touch. **Lead with the real thing; let the wink ride on
top.** Keep it to a sentence or two — the card also has rules text, and flavour must never be mistaken
for it. The affection is in the specificity.

> "'You're too thin!' She won't take no." *(A bad night for saying no; the auntie wins.)*
> "You said you were full three plates ago; she's already reaching for the ladle."
> "You weren't hungry; you bought a 茶葉蛋 anyway."
> "The whole coast smells like a smoothie." *(Mango Season)*

### Rules text — *clear and warm, NOT jokey*
Teaching comes first. Warm and plain, never a legal document — but the comedy lives in *flavour*, not
in the rules. A player parsing what to do must not have to parse a joke to do it. **The wit is the
seasoning on top of an instruction that is already unambiguous.** (Rules *wording that changes meaning*
is `bg-elegance` / `bg-design`'s lane — you polish presentation, you don't rewrite the rule.)

> "Return 1 of your followers to your OWN supply (your crowd disperses — it does NOT join the rival)."

Clear first (what you do), warm gloss in the parenthesis (why it feels okay), no punchline getting in
the way. Note the binding **Followers wording rail** from `identity.md`: frame any loss as *the crowd
ebbing / drifting to a rival's stall* — **never** a bare "−N Followers" (a real social-media feel-bad
is the one way this warm beat curdles).

### Marketing / box copy — *hooky, punchy, sells the fantasy*
The loudest volume. Sell the picture — two rival agents, one night market, the last train. Punchy,
rhythmic, in-voice. Feeds `bg-marketing`; you supply the voice, they own the campaign framing.

> *Two rival travel agents. One night market. One last train home. Grab your trench coat.*

## 3. The gag layer — the secret-agent wink (how to keep it warm)

The premise is behavioural wordplay: they're **travel agents** who behave like **secret agents** about
it. The whole comic engine is that gap. Two devices carry it and both **land on the agent, never the
place**:

- **The mission-dossier voice.** Head office on the earpiece; "Operation: Beef Noodle"; a typhoon is a
  *comic inconvenience head office groans about on the phone*, never peril. A warm caper, not a
  thriller — the fizz of a game-show, never danger or dread.
- **The "marketing opportunity" running gag** (the load-bearing cushion on the word **Bribe**). The
  agent theatrically slides a "bribe" across the table — *"this isn't a bribe, it's a **marketing
  opportunity**."* What's actually on offer is free publicity, a glowing review, and a busload of
  hungry tourists — a windfall the vendor is **delighted** to accept. The smarm lands on the agent; the
  vendor freely picks whichever agent's driving the bigger crowd. Never *acquire / control / own /
  corrupt* a vendor — always *win over / feature / champion / put on the poster*. This gag recurs
  wherever "Bribe" appears; it's funny *and* it's the guardrail.

## 4. Techniques (the moves that produce the voice)

1. **Rule-of-three escalation.** Three beats, each raising the stakes of something small, until it's
   gloriously out of proportion. *"His mother packed 'a few snacks.' The backpack now weighs more than
   he does."*
2. **The deadpan turn.** After the escalation, one flat, matter-of-fact line that treats the absurd as
   obvious. *"Nobody knows how. Nobody asks."* The comedy is in refusing to be surprised.
3. **Hyper-specificity.** Name the real thing — 茶葉蛋, 珍奶, the 3:47 to Zuoying, Dihua Street at dawn.
   A specific true detail is warmer, funnier, and more anti-generic than any invented one. Vagueness is
   the enemy; specificity is the affection.
4. **Affectionate exaggeration.** Blow up the *character's* trait, never the culture's. The Globetrotter
   is in three cities by lunch; the auntie just won't take no. The exaggeration inflates the agent, and
   the local stays real.
5. **The wink at the strategy.** A great line quietly hints *why the character or card is good to play*
   — without stating a rule. *"He's the only guide who never wastes time shopping because Mum already
   thought of everything"* tells you the Homebody's edge is sourcing, in pure voice. The flavour teaches
   a little; the rules text still owns the actual rule.

## 5. Do / Don't (concrete boundaries)

| ✅ DO | ❌ DON'T |
|---|---|
| "'You're too thin!' She won't take no. Take 1 free Seafood." — the auntie is warm, generous, real. | "The pushy local forces food on you." — makes the local a caricature / an obstacle. **The #1 don't.** |
| "This isn't a bribe, it's a marketing opportunity." — the joke is the *agent's* spy-caper self-image. | "You bribe the corrupt vendor." — makes the vendor corrupt; inverts the whole rail. |
| "The whole coast smells like a smoothie." — specific, sensory, true (Mango Season). | "Exotic tropical fruits abound!" — generic, touristic, an outsider's gaze. |
| "Nobody's quite sure what he actually does. They just know everybody knows him." — warm rogue, deadpan. | "He's a gangster who makes people afraid." — menace; breaks the warm-鄰居-大哥 read. |
| "You cut a corner and it showed — some of the crowd drifts elsewhere." — rueful shrug. | "You FAILED. Lose 2 Followers." — scold + a bare social-media feel-bad. |
| "One more 關東煮 for the road." — the specific convenience-store staple, told plainly. | "Grab some snacks at the local mini-mart." — flattened, generic, no affection. |
| A CANCELLED tour is *"ah, that one didn't run in time."* | A CANCELLED tour is *"failed / dead / lost."* — punitive register the piles' names guard against. |

**The through-line:** every DON'T either makes the place/people/food the butt, turns warm into
menacing, or trades a specific true detail for a generic one. Those are the three ways the voice breaks.

## 6. Binding rails (voice-level)

Carry these in every pass. They cross-reference `identity.md` (§Tone & vibe, §Naming & voice,
§Sensitivity) and `art-style.md`.

- **English name first.** In prose and docs, the English / plain-language name leads; the
  original-language name (繁體 / Tâi-lô) follows in parentheses only where precision or the
  sensitivity/component-data context needs it — e.g. "the Homebody (媽寶)". Maintain the canonical
  name table with `bg-continuity` (lives in `identity.md` §Naming & voice + `names.md`).
- **Traditional characters, never Simplified.** Every 繁體 string is Traditional. Taiwan uses
  Traditional.
- **Secular, always.** Food fairs, fruit seasons, weather, roads, crowds, characters — never temple
  festivals, deities, ghost-month, or funerary customs, even as decoration or a gag. (Where a real
  festival is named — Dragon-Boat, Mid-Autumn, Lantern — keep it **races + food + lanterns, no rites**,
  exactly as the booking flavour already does.)
- **Dignified locals + faithful real dishes/places.** Vendors, aunties, and cooks are people with
  dignity, warm and specific, never obstacles or exotic punchlines. Name real dishes and places
  faithfully and specifically; never mangle a dish name to fit a gag; keep vendors **dish-typed generic
  stalls**, never a real named business printed with an accolade it doesn't hold.
- **The two Indigenous dishes stay consultant-gated.** Any Indigenous name/term is flagged to
  `bg-publishing` for a paid community consultant before final/print. Don't write final copy around
  them until cleared.
- **The two load-bearing character reads** (blessed *only while warm*): keep **The Homebody (媽寶)**
  affectionate-not-mocking and **The Big Brother (大哥)** a warm neighbourhood rogue, never a menacing
  gangster or an Orwellian "Big Brother." The Big Brother is the #1 name consultant watch-item — the
  English doesn't automatically carry the warm 大哥 sense, so the *voice* (and art) must.

## 8. Lane note (so this file stays in its lane)

- `bg-theme` owns the **naming/voice identity** (the register, which names are locked); this file
  *writes in* that voice and proposes names that fit — it doesn't set the identity.
- `bg-elegance` owns **rules wording that changes meaning** and keeps rulebook/aid text tight;
  `bg-rulebook` owns the rulebook as a document. This file supplies flavour + component copy.
- `bg-marketing` owns **selling/positioning**; this file feeds it a consistent voice + box lines.
- `bg-continuity` proofreads and guards term/name consistency across everything — write to the
  canonical name table so continuity has less to catch.
- **The hard boundary:** this file governs *presentation words only*. A mechanics/balance/rules-logic
  problem surfaced by copy work gets **flagged** to `bg-design` / `bg-elegance` / `bg-critic` /
  `bg-playtest` — never fixed here.
