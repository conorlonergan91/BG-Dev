# ✍️ Travel Agents: Taiwan Food Tour — Prose Identity (the VOICE BIBLE)

> The living source of truth for the game's **voice** — the way every player-facing word sounds.
> Third canonical reference alongside **`identity.md`** (the felt/game identity) and

> rule's meaning (that's `bg-design` / `bg-elegance`).
>
> **Citation convention (read this first, use it every time).** Every copy or writing brief in this

## 1. The core voice — one statement

**An over-caffeinated race-commentator who's madly in love with Taiwanese food — calling the elbows-out
scramble between rival agents at full volume, and going soft and reverent the second the food comes out.**

Eight adjectives, two jobs: loud about the **rivalry** — **raucous · over-the-top · competitive ·
absurd**; warm about the **island** — **warm · affectionate · specific · celebratory.** Big and gleeful,
never dry.

**The single most important rule — the one that governs everything else:**

> **The joke always lands on the over-the-top travel-agent character — and on their rivals. Never on the
> locals, the vendors, the food, or the culture — those stay dignified, real, and beloved.**

We laugh *with* Taiwan, never *at* it. The comedy is six grown adults treating a bowl of noodles like the
final lap of a demolition derby — snatching stalls out from under each other, hyping their busloads louder
than the next. The auntie pressing a third ladle on you, the oyster omelette, the 完售 sold-out sign, the
last train bearing down — those are true, warm, and told straight (or told with awe). The gag is the
*agents'* full-contact scramble over them.

**The one test** (from `identity.md`, load-bearing): *would a Taiwanese player laugh **with** this, or
feel it laughs **at** their home?* If a line only works because you find Taiwan itself strange or
foreign, cut it. **Secular always** — no temple/deity/ghost-month/funerary motifs as decoration or gags.

## 2. Tone by surface

Each writing surface holds the *same* narrator but at a slightly different volume. One-liner + a
gold-standard example for each.

### Character mat quote — *the cocky one-liner*
A single line in the character's own voice. A brag, a boast, a battle-cry — the humour in how seriously
they take a food fight. **One line. No setup, no explanation.**

> "You call it over-packing. Mum calls it being prepared." *(The Homebody)*
> "I've eaten this whole island. Twice. Good luck keeping a stall from me." *(The Big Eater)*
> "Keep up if you can, dear — I already booked tomorrow." *(The Globetrotter)*

### Character backstory — *play-by-play escalation* (the gold standard)
Short lines. **Rule-of-three escalation**, called like a race, until it's gloriously out of proportion —
then **end on a wink** that also hints why they're good to play. The character is affectionately
ridiculous and hell-bent on winning; the food and the island stay warm and real. This is the register the
CD wants — mine it.

> AND SHE'S OFF — first stall, cleared. Second stall, cleared. Third stall, she's already eating the
> fourth while a rival's *still* reading the menu.
>
> Half the field is carrying food babies and quietly questioning their life choices. She's flagging down
> the dessert cart.
>
> Folks, she has personally out-eaten every rival on this island. Several times. Do NOT get between this
> woman and a beef-noodle stall. *(The Big Eater)*

Note the shape: three escalating beats called at volume → a raucous mid-line that treats the absurd as
obvious ("food babies") → the wink that **hints why she's good to play** ("out-eaten every rival … do
NOT get between her and a stall"). That last move is §4's *wink at the strategy*.

### Card flavour (events / bookings / vendors) — *the caller's aside, specific and short*
One grounded Taiwan detail — reverent when it's the food, loud when it's the rivalry. **Lead with the
real thing; let the call ride on top.** Keep it to a sentence or two — the card also has rules text, and
flavour must never be mistaken for it. The affection is in the specificity.

> "The whole coast smells like a smoothie — and every agent on the island just turned their head." *(Mango Season)*
> "'You're too thin!' She won't take no. Free plate — grab it before your rival does." *(the auntie wins; everybody wants in.)*
> "You weren't hungry. You bought a 茶葉蛋 anyway. No notes." *(a small true joy, told straight.)*
> "Sold out. 完售. Someone got there first — and they are NOT sorry." *(the groan, played for laughs.)*

### Rules text — *clear and warm, NOT jokey*
Teaching comes first. Warm and plain, never a legal document — but the comedy lives in *flavour*, not in
the rules. A player parsing what to do must not have to parse a joke to do it. **The wit is the seasoning
on top of an instruction that is already unambiguous.** (Rules *wording that changes meaning* is
`bg-elegance` / `bg-design`'s lane — you polish presentation, you don't rewrite the rule.)

> "Return 1 of your followers to your OWN supply (your crowd disperses — it does NOT join the rival)."

Clear first (what you do), warm gloss in the parenthesis (why it feels okay), no punchline getting in the
way. Note the binding **Followers wording rail** from `identity.md`: frame any loss as *the crowd
ebbing / drifting to a rival's stall* — **never** a bare "−N Followers" (a real social-media feel-bad is
the one way this warm beat curdles).

### Marketing / box copy — *hooky, punchy, sells the fantasy*
The loudest volume. Sell the picture — a pack of rival agents, one night market, the last train, elbows
out. Punchy, rhythmic, in-voice. Feeds `bg-marketing`; you supply the voice, they own the campaign
framing.

> *Six rival agents. One island of legendary food. One eating day to out-hype them all — and the last train's already coming. GO.*

## 3. The gag layer — the rivalry engine (how to keep it warm)

The premise is a full-contact race: they're **travel agents** who behave like they're driving the last
lap of a demolition derby about it. The whole comic engine is that gap — a wholesome job (showing off the
island's best food) fought over like a bloodsport. Two devices carry it, and both **land on the agents,
never the place**:

- **The play-by-play call.** Narrate the mayhem like a sports commentator who's also head-over-heels for
  the food: *"she just SNATCHED the beef-noodle king out from under him!"* The rivalry is loud and in the
  frame — cut-offs, snatches, busloads shouted louder than the next. A typhoon is a *comic
  inconvenience the whole field groans about*, never peril; the fizz is a game-show, never danger or
  dread. Loud about the fight, reverent about the bowl.
- **The "marketing opportunity" running gag** (the load-bearing cushion on the word **Bribe**). The agent
  theatrically slides a "bribe" across the table — *"this isn't a bribe, it's a **marketing
  opportunity**."* What's actually on offer is free publicity, a glowing review, and a busload of hungry
  tourists — a windfall the vendor is **delighted** to accept. The smarm lands on the agent; the vendor
  freely picks whichever agent's driving the bigger crowd. Never *acquire / control / own / corrupt* a
  vendor — always *win over / feature / champion / put on the poster*. This gag recurs wherever "Bribe"
  appears; it's funny *and* it's the guardrail.

## 4. Techniques (the moves that produce the voice)

1. **Rule-of-three escalation.** Three beats, each raising the stakes of something small, until it's
   gloriously out of proportion. *"His mother packed 'a few snacks.' The backpack now weighs more than he
   does. He's winning."*
2. **The play-by-play call.** Narrate the small thing like a live race — present tense, breathless, caps
   for the big move. *"He reaches for the last 關東煮 — and SO DOES SHE."* The comedy is calling a food
   fight like the final lap.
3. **Hyper-specificity.** Name the real thing — 茶葉蛋, 珍奶, the 3:47 to Zuoying, Dihua Street at dawn. A
   specific true detail is warmer, funnier, and more anti-generic than any invented one. Vagueness is the
   enemy; specificity is the affection. (This is where the voice goes *reverent* — the food is always
   named straight.)
4. **Affectionate exaggeration.** Blow up the *agents'* traits and their rivalry, never the culture's.
   The Globetrotter is in three cities by lunch; the Big Eater's cleared the stall before you sat down.
   The exaggeration inflates the agents, and the local stays real.
5. **The wink at the strategy.** A great line quietly hints *why the character or card is good to play* —
   without stating a rule. *"He never wastes a second shopping — Mum already thought of everything"* tells
   you the Homebody's edge is sourcing, in pure voice. The flavour teaches a little; the rules text still
   owns the actual rule.

## 5. Do / Don't (concrete boundaries)

| ✅ DO | ❌ DON'T |
|---|---|
| "'You're too thin!' She won't take no. Take 1 free Seafood — before your rival does." — the auntie is warm and real; the fight is between agents. | "The pushy local forces food on you." — makes the local a caricature / an obstacle. **The #1 don't.** |
| "This isn't a bribe, it's a marketing opportunity." — the joke is the *agent's* over-the-top hustle. | "You bribe the corrupt vendor." — makes the vendor corrupt; inverts the whole rail. |
| "The whole coast smells like a smoothie — and every agent just turned their head." — specific, sensory, true, then loud. | "Exotic tropical fruits abound!" — generic, touristic, an outsider's gaze. |
| "She just SNATCHED the beef-noodle king out from under him!" — elbows-out, aimed agent-at-agent, played for laughs. | "He's a gangster who makes people afraid." — menace; breaks the warm-鄰居-老大 read. |
| "You cut a corner and it showed — some of the crowd drifts to a rival's stall." — rueful, no scold. | "You FAILED. Lose 2 Followers." — scold + a bare social-media feel-bad. |
| "One more 關東煮 for the road." — the specific convenience-store staple, told plainly. | "Grab some snacks at the local mini-mart." — flattened, generic, no affection. |
| A CANCELLED tour is *"ah, that one didn't run in time."* | A CANCELLED tour is *"failed / dead / lost."* — punitive register the piles' names guard against. |

**The through-line:** every DON'T either makes the place/people/food the butt, turns the loud rivalry
*mean* (menace, not game-show fizz), or trades a specific true detail for a generic one. Those are the
three ways the voice breaks.

## 6. Binding rails (voice-level)

Carry these in every pass. They cross-reference `identity.md` (§Tone & vibe, §Naming & voice,
§Sensitivity) and `art-style.md`.

- **English name first.** In prose and docs, the English / plain-language name leads; the
  original-language name (繁體 / Tâi-lô) follows in parentheses only where precision or the
  sensitivity/component-data context needs it — e.g. "the Homebody (媽寶)". Maintain the canonical name
  table with `bg-continuity` (lives in `identity.md` §Naming & voice + `names.md`).
- **Traditional characters, never Simplified.** Every 繁體 string is Traditional. Taiwan uses Traditional.
- **Secular, always.** Food fairs, fruit seasons, weather, roads, crowds, characters — never temple
  festivals, deities, ghost-month, or funerary customs, even as decoration or a gag. (Where a real
  festival is named — Dragon-Boat, Mid-Autumn, Lantern — keep it **races + food + lanterns, no rites**,
  exactly as the booking flavour already does.)
- **Dignified locals + faithful real dishes/places.** Vendors, aunties, and cooks are people with
  dignity, warm and specific, never obstacles or exotic punchlines. Name real dishes and places
  faithfully and specifically; never mangle a dish name to fit a gag; keep vendors **dish-typed generic
  stalls**, never a real named business printed with an accolade it doesn't hold.
- **The two Indigenous dishes stay consultant-gated.** Any Indigenous name/term is flagged to
  `bg-publishing` for a paid community consultant before final/print. Don't write final copy around them
  until cleared.
- **The two load-bearing character reads** (blessed *only while warm*): keep **The Homebody (媽寶)**
  affectionate-not-mocking and **The Big Boss (老大)** a warm, affectionate fixer, never a menacing
  gangster or a triad/gang caricature. The Big Boss is the #1 name consultant watch-item — 老大 carries a
  boss/gang-leader connotation, so the warm-fixer read does NOT come for free; the *voice* (and art) must
  carry it, and the portrayal is **consultant-gated** before final/print.

## 8. Lane note (so this file stays in its lane)

- `bg-theme` owns the **naming/voice identity** (the register, which names are locked); this file
  *writes in* that voice and proposes names that fit — it doesn't set the identity.
- `bg-elegance` owns **rules wording that changes meaning** and keeps rulebook/aid text tight;
  `bg-rulebook` owns the rulebook as a document. This file supplies flavour + component copy.
- `bg-marketing` owns **selling/positioning**; this file feeds it a consistent voice + box lines.
- `bg-continuity` proofreads and guards term/name consistency across everything — write to the canonical
  name table so continuity has less to catch.
- **The hard boundary:** this file governs *presentation words only*. A mechanics/balance/rules-logic
  problem surfaced by copy work gets **flagged** to `bg-design` / `bg-elegance` / `bg-critic` /
  `bg-playtest` — never fixed here.
