# Rulebook figures — illustration briefs

> One brief per figure code placed in `design/rules-draft.md`. These are **briefs for a human illustrator
> (or a clearly-labelled placeholder mockup)** — per the studio rails there is **NO AI final art**; do not
> generate finished art from these. Style throughout: the **night-market identity** (`identity.md` §Look &
> feel) — warm dusk palette (lantern vermilion `#D64545`, jade teal `#1F8A7A`, golden amber `#E6A930`,
> dusk indigo `#26324D`, bao-paper cream `#F5EFE2`), hand-painted market-signage warmth, passport /
> boarding-pass shape language, rounded corners. Suits always carry **colour + emblem**, never colour
> alone (colour-blind safe). All Chinese is **繁體字** (Traditional). Tone: sunny, funny, welcoming — a
> figure a first-time player relaxes at, never a technical schematic.

## [FIG-SETUP] — the annotated table layout (the hero figure)
**Where:** §2 Setup. **Job:** replace a wall of setup prose — a player should build the table from this
picture plus the bullets.

**Subject:** a top-down (or gentle 3/4) view of a set-up 2-player game at dusk — the Taiwan region-ring
board, the two ingredient/vendor decks per region, the tracks, and the Buzz supply, with two agent pawns
sitting together in Taipei.

**Must show + number these annotations (match the bullets exactly):**
- **① region decks** — each of the four regions (North 北 · Central 中 · South 南 · East 東) with its
  face-down **ingredient deck** and face-down **vendor deck**; the North↔South **HSR line** drawn across the
  ring.
- **② vendor slots** — the face-up vendor slots filled: **2 in the North, 1 in each other region.**
- **③ Reach & Tours-Completed tracks** — the two per-player track markers, Reach set to **2**, Tours
  Completed set to **0** (show the Rave-Review marks at 2 and 4 on the Tours track).
- **④ Buzz supply** — the shared pool of Buzz tokens off to the side.
- A callout label **"everyone starts in Taipei"** with both pawns on the North hub.

**Notes:** keep region colours warm and dual-coded (region emblem + 繁體 label on each). Show the DAY layer
face-up and hint at the NIGHT piles set aside. Friendly numbered roundels (passport-stamp style) for ①–④.

## [FIG-TURN] — the six actions, at a glance
**Where:** §3 Your turn. **Job:** a scannable icon row so the six actions read without re-reading prose.

**Subject:** six small warm icon tiles in a single row (or 2×3 grid), each with its keyword label:
1. **Visit the Local Market** — a hand flipping 3 ingredient cards.
2. **Convenience Store** — a 24-hr storefront, one card flipped anywhere.
3. **Travel** — a pawn hopping between regions along the HSR, with a small **+1 Reach** badge.
4. **Promote** — 2 Buzz tokens dropping onto a vendor card.
5. **Bribe a Vendor** — an agent sliding a "marketing opportunity" across a stall counter (warm, comedic).
6. **Line Up a Tour Ticket** — a hand taking a boarding-pass ticket from the row.

**Notes:** each icon language-independent first, label second. Keep the Bribe icon unmistakably warm/cheeky
(trench-coat-over-Hawaiian-shirt agent), never sinister — the vendor is delighted.

## [FIG-REACH] — your hype can't outrun your Reach
**Where:** §4 Buzz & Reach. **Job:** show the cap relationship in one image so the rule needs little prose.

**Subject:** a vendor (or two) with a visible stack of the player's Buzz tokens on it, and beside it the
player's **Reach marker** on its track. A clear visual equation / gauge: **committed Buzz ≤ Reach.** Show
one "OK" state (Buzz stack under the Reach line) and one "not allowed" state (a stack that would exceed
Reach, crossed out) — and a small inset of **winning the vendor → its Buzz clears → the cap frees up.**

**Notes:** Buzz in the violet hue (`#6A4C93`); Reach marker matches its track. Keep it a friendly gauge,
not a math diagram.

---

## [FIG-BRIBE] — the three-step Bribe
**Where:** §5 Bribing a vendor. **Job:** the three conditions + the payoff as a mini flow.

**Subject:** a small left-to-right, three-panel strip ending in a payoff:
1. **Be there** — the agent's pawn standing in the vendor's region.
2. **Out-Buzz by 2** — your Buzz stack on the vendor is at least 2 higher than the rival's (show both
   stacks with the +2 gap).
3. **Pay the ingredients** — ingredient chips paid, minus region-icon discounts (show one icon crossing out
   one chip).
→ **Payoff:** the vendor card sliding onto the player's tour as a **stop**, its Buzz clearing to the supply,
and the day-clock ticking **+1**.

**Notes:** show a region icon on the won vendor with a little "next one's cheaper" spark to hint at the
engine ramp. Warm celebration, not a transaction.

## [FIG-SCOPE] — who an event hits (⦿ / ⬢ / ➤)
**Where:** §6 Markets & events. **Job:** teach the three event scopes as dual-coded glyphs.

**Subject:** three little region-map vignettes, each with its drawn glyph (never a font glyph — draw the
shapes, colour-blind safe):
- **⦿ You** — a single figure highlighted (only the flipper).
- **⬢ This region** — everyone standing in the drawn region highlighted.
- **➤ Next door** — the effect arrow spilling into a neighbouring region.

Include a small separate callout for **排班保養 Scheduled Maintenance** in its **own island-wide banner
frame** (visibly NOT a ⦿/⬢/➤ card): the HSR line crossed out, both pawns rerouting the long way round the
ring.

**Notes:** keep hazards warm/secular (a typhoon day, a 完售 sold-out sign, a festival crowd) — a shared
groan, never a spiteful strike.

## [FIG-CLOCK] — the day-clock: Day → Night
**Where:** §7 Day → Night. **Job:** show the single ascending clock and its two marks.

**Subject:** a horizontal day-clock track climbing left→right from morning to night, with two labelled
marks: **DUSK** (2p: the 5th vendor win — the street flips from warm-amber day to neon night market) and
**LAST TRAIN** (2p: the 10th — a departing train, "catch the last train home"). A small "+1 each Bribe"
badge on the track. Show the warm-amber **Act I 白天** half and the neon **Act II 夜市** half, with the dusk
flip in the middle (day layer sweeping away, night stalls arriving).

**Notes:** warm, not alarm — dusk is a giddy payoff, the last train a fond wind-down. Day = amber/gold;
night = festive lantern-neon (lavender/magenta), never a cold/red alarm register.
